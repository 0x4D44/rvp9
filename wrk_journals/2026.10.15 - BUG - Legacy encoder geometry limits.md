# RVP9-BUG-KILN-00071: legacy encoder geometry limits

Manual bug-drain fix, claimed as `fix-20261015T222840Z-dc59d6d8` in its own fresh
worktree after landing RVP9-BUG-KILN-00069.

The legacy `Encoder::new` checked alignment and nonzero dimensions but omitted the
streaming encoder's size envelope. A 524,288-square configuration was accepted,
then its 65,536-square mode grid overflowed a `u32` product during encoding.

The constructor now uses the streaming encoder's existing
`coded_dimensions_supported` predicate: each dimension at most 16,384, at most
8,192 squared luma pixels. It also computes checked plane lengths, mode-grid and
superblock counts, coefficient storage sizes, tile capacities, packet capacity,
and the two-frame superframe capacity before encoding. Geometry retained in the
encoder replaces unchecked allocation products in the legacy paths. Existing
alignment, nonzero-size and quantizer validation stays in place.

## Regression evidence

The tests invoke only the allocation-free constructor. No giant frame buffers
are constructed or encoded.

- Before the fix, the original 524,288-square constructor regression executed and
  failed its expected-rejection assertion.
- The finalized dimension regression independently tries 16,448x64 and 64x16,448,
  and accepts the exact 16,384x4,096 dimension/area boundary. Without the fix, it
  executed and failed the oversized-width rejection assertion.
- The finalized area regression tries 16,384x16,384, within the dimension caps but
  above the luma cap. Without the fix, it executed and failed the area-rejection
  assertion.

The shared rate-control predicate's behavior is unchanged; only its crate-local
visibility changed. Independent closure remains for another verifier after
landing.
