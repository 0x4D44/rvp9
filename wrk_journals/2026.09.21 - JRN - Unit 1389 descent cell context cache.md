# Unit 1389 — A4 descent cell context cache

## Decision

Keep the candidate. It caches the A4 descent picker’s above/left-derived
context once per 8x8 cell, while retaining block-size-dependent motion
reference lookup in the callback. Two exact Crucible screens agree on a small
whole-encode gain, and every tested RVP9 stream is byte-identical to control.

This is a Tesco-sized improvement, not parity. The candidate remains about
1.4057× slower than the pinned libvpx encoder on the paired three-clip corpus,
so the parity gap is still open for later units.

## Why this candidate

The integrated x86-64 profile at `a94c8ce8` attributes 12.543% of 19,597,341
weighted RVP9 user samples to
`pick_and_simulate_inter_encode_partitions::closure$10`. The callback repeatedly
derived reference costs, interpolation context, filter reference, skip context,
and neighbouring motion vectors from the same 8x8 cell. The cache computes those
values lazily per cell and leaves block-size-dependent work unchanged.

## Correctness evidence

- `cargo fmt --all -- --check` and `git diff --check` passed.
- The focused test `descent_cell_picker_context_matches_direct_neighbours`
  passed.
- Deliberate red run: changing the cached `skip_ctx` to `get_pred_context_skip(...)
  + 1` failed with `left: 2 right: 1`; restoring the exact calculation returned
  the test to green.
- The local AArch64 encoder library passed 780 tests, with one ignored.
- The x86-64 cross-test build (`x86_64-apple-darwin`) passed.
- Crucible release builds completed for control and candidate.
- Both exact performance screens produced one stable, identical stream hash per
  RVP9 clip and arm. The change is therefore speed-only for these inputs.

## Crucible screens

Both screens used CPU-used 6, CBR 1260 kbps, 30 fps, one thread, zero tile
columns, AQ 0, zero noise sensitivity, 60 frames, KF interval 60, one warmup,
15 alternating pairs, two reversed clip-order rotations, and no `--max-load1`
cap. Ratios are candidate/control; lower is faster.

| screen | clip | separate | paired | candidate ms/frame | control ms/frame |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | ducks | 1.003993× | 1.003295× | 19.1298 | 19.0538 |
| A | in-to-tree | 0.981580× | 0.994880× | 12.3579 | 12.5899 |
| A | old-town-cross | 0.733356× | 0.993778× | 18.4942 | 25.2186 |
| A | three-clip corpus | 0.897409× | 0.997308× | — | — |
| B | ducks | 1.028400× | 0.999938× | 19.9530 | 19.4019 |
| B | in-to-tree | 0.999073× | 0.997192× | 12.0794 | 12.0906 |
| B | old-town-cross | 0.992331× | 0.993004× | 10.9198 | 11.0042 |
| B | three-clip corpus | 1.006480× | 0.996708× | — | — |

The paired corpus ratio is below 1.0× on both runs (0.997308× and 0.996708×).
Screen A saw high unrelated host load (about 18–23 busy logical cores), while
screen B was quiet (about 0.4–3.0); the agreement makes the small gain usable
under the relaxed load policy.

The pinned libvpx comparison used the same protocol and inputs:
