# Track E3 synthesis — the bitrate overshoot is the trunk, not motion comp

Date: 2026-05-22.
Author: night-7 team-lead (Claude).

## E3 verdict (commit 7c493ff)

Diagnosed *why* rvp9 picks 0% NEWMV on foreman. Discriminated three
hypotheses with env-gated picker instrumentation (`RVP9_E3_MVDIAG`):

- **NOT** "ME unwired" — `pick_inter_mode_luma` is invoked per leaf;
  NEWMV is searched; returns nonzero MVs 99–100% of leaves.
- **NOT** "ME returns near-zero MVs" — MVs are nonzero (avg mag 80–194
  1/8-pel).
- Surface cause is **RD imbalance** (NEWMV's MV-rate term, correct and
  libvpx-faithful, always beats its tiny distortion saving) — BUT the
  decisive cross-check kills the cheap fix: forcing q low (λ≈0, MV bits
  effectively free) STILL yields 0% NEWMV, because on foreman the
  **(0,0) LASTFRAME predictor is already near-perfect frame-to-frame at
  30 fps**. NEWMV saves ~12 SSE/block (median 7) — noise. Lowering the
  MV-cost weight will not move BD-rate.

So **motion compensation is NOT the foreman lever**, and 0% NEWMV is a
*symptom* of q=255 saturation, not a cause. (Contributing defect logged:
`int_pro_motion_estimation` returns the `u32::MAX` bail sentinel on 12/16
SBs — every edge SB of a 256×256 frame. Real bug, but not the lever.)

## The synthesis across E1 + E2 + E3

A surprising, coherent picture:

1. **Foreman inter residuals are genuinely small** (E3): ZEROMV against
   LASTFRAME is near-perfect at 30 fps.
2. **Yet rvp9 spends 2.3× libvpx's inter-frame bits** at a *higher* Q
   (E1: 8222 vs 3565 bits/frame at 100k; rvp9 avg q 211 vs libvpx 204).
3. If residuals are small but bits are 2.3×, the excess must be **coding
   overhead**, not residual energy:
   - **Over-partitioning** (E1): 85% → 77% 8×8 after E2; each leaf pays
     mode + skip + partition + context syntax 30–60× too often.
   - **Insufficient skip** (hypothesis): rvp9 codes tiny residual
     coefficients on blocks libvpx would skip outright.
4. That overhead is a **bit floor** → bitrate overshoot (250 vs 143 kbps
   at 100k target) → RC pushes Q up → at high Q the VBP thresholds
   collapse (E2's ceiling) and λ explodes (E3's NEWMV death). Everything
   downstream is gated by the overshoot.

**The trunk is the bitrate overshoot.** E2 trimmed one branch
(partition thresholds, +27 pp) but hit the q=255 ceiling. E3 ruled out
the motion-comp branch entirely. The remaining question is *what* makes
the bit floor so high, and *why the RC overshoots instead of raising Q*.

## Open question → Track E4 (task #65)

Pin the overshoot mechanism, rvp9-side (no dependency on the open decoder
mi-grid mode-field bug):

1. **Bit decomposition** of rvp9's ~8222 inter-frame bits: coefficient
   vs per-block syntax (mode/skip/partition) vs MV vs header. Where does
   the budget actually go?
2. **RC overshoot**: per-frame target vs rate-model-predicted vs actual
   bits. Is the rate model under-predicting (so RC under-raises Q)? Does
   RC hit a Q ceiling below 255 at 100k (E1 showed max q=243, not 255)?
3. **Skip utilisation**: what fraction does rvp9 skip vs what it *could*
   skip (residual energy below the skip threshold)? Is it coding
   near-zero residuals it should drop?

E4 is diagnostic-only. Its result is the genuine **multi-week-vs-ship
decision point**: if the overshoot is dominated by a tractable
coding/skip/RC fix (à la E2), Track A finishes cheaply; if it needs a
rate-model + block_yrd rebuild, that's Arthur's multi-week call.
