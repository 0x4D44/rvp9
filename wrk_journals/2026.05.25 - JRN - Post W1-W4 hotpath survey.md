# Post-W1+W4 hot-path survey

**Date:** 2026-05-25 (evening, post W1 + W4 + W2-opt-in)
**Author:** profile agent (read-only on source)
**Goal:** identify the top 3 hot spots in the rvp9 decoder NOW that IDCT (W1) and per-frame allocator traffic (W4) are largely off the table; size Wave 5+.

## TL;DR

| Stage at `testsrc_1080p30 t=4` | µs / frame (median, post-W1+W4) | % of frame |
|---|---:|---:|
| **loopfilter** (incl. mask setup + filter apply, serial on main thread) | **1 780** | **54.7%** |
| **join** (main blocked on 4 parallel tile workers) | 1 131 | 34.8% |
| merge (per-tile rectangle copy into frame YV12 + seg_ids) | 85 | 2.6% |
| setup (per-tile-slot alloc, pre-scope) | 85 | 2.6% |
| border (`extend_frame_borders`) | 85 | 2.6% |
| submit (worker scope fan-out) | 16 | 0.5% |
| grid_stitch | 0 | 0.0% (folded into tile workers via Phase 2 shared-MI grid) |
| (tile_decode, parallel work, sum of 4 tiles) | 4 348 | (133.6%) |
| **Median wall-time per frame** | **3 256** | **100%** |

Sum of named main-thread phases = 3 182 µs; closes to wall 3 256 µs within 2.3%.
Implies tile_decode finishes ahead of LF on the critical path; `join` is short relative to median tile_decode (4348 / 4 = 1087 µs) — main spends ~1131 µs waiting then ~1780 µs in LF.

**The serial loopfilter on the main thread is now the single largest item on the critical path.** That is the same loopfilter Wave 2 tried to parallelise; the row-MT attempt failed because the scope-overhead exceeded the gain. We need a different lever on the *same* hot stage.

Big caveats:
- The T-5 baseline (2026-05-24, *pre*-W1+W4) had LF at 35.5% / 3 448 µs and grid_stitch at 6.8% / 663 µs of a 9 716 µs frame. Phase 2 (shared MI grid) eliminated grid_stitch; W1+W4 cut frame time from ~9.7 ms to ~3.3 ms; LF cycles dropped from 3 448 µs to 1 780 µs (**-48%** in absolute terms — partly because shared-MI grid eliminated frame_mi materialisation cost too, partly because the `Decoder::new` setup/border numbers are now stable).
- So contrary to my prompt's wording, **LF as a percentage went UP, not down**, because everything else shrank faster. In absolute µs it's down by half.
- All four LF kernels (v4/h4/v8/h8/v16/h16, both single and dual) already dispatch to AVX2 implementations on AVX2 CPUs. There is no scalar-to-SIMD lever left in the LF kernels themselves.

### What's missing from the bench harness

**The kernel categories needed for a *complete* Wave 5 sizing are NOT covered by `dsp-microbench`:**

- ❌ `convolve8_horiz` (only `_vert` and `_2D` are timed)
- ❌ IDCT kernels (the W1 path) — no ns/op available; only via end-to-end bench
- ❌ inverse-quantize (`dequant_buf`) — no per-call timing
- ❌ loop-filter edge filters (`lpf_horizontal_{4,8,16}`, `lpf_vertical_{4,8,16}`, ±_dual) — *the biggest serial cost on the frame and yet not benched per-kernel*
- ❌ intra predictors TM_PRED, DC, V, H, D45, D135, D63, D207

This is a gap. Recommend a follow-up to extend `dsp-microbench` with LF edge kernels (highest priority given today's findings).

## 3. Cross-reference: kernels × stages × call counts

Estimating calls/frame at 1080p (1920×1080 = 32 400 luma 8×8 cells; ~8 100 chroma 4:2:0):

| Stage | Dominant kernel(s) | Calls/frame (1080p) | Time/call (avx2) | Estimated stage µs | Match? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| loopfilter | lpf_{horiz,vert}_{4,8,16}{,_dual}, mostly 16 on SB-edges | ~6 000 edges (luma) + 1 500 chroma = ~7 500 calls | ~200 ns/edge (per libvpx microbenches, no local data) | ~1 500 µs | within 16% of measured 1 780 µs ✓ |
| inter prediction (inside tile_decode) | convolve8_2D 8x8 .. 32x32, weighted to 8x8/16x16 | ~3 000-4 000 MC calls (depends on partition density) | ~100-700 ns per call | ~800-1 200 µs | plausible — fits inside 4 348 µs tile_decode/4-cores ≈ 1 087 µs/tile |
| inverse transform | IDCT 4/8/16/32 (W1 sparse paths) | ~3 000 transforms | ~100-500 ns | ~600-900 µs | plausible portion of tile_decode |
| inverse quantize | quant scalar (probably) | ~32 400 per plane × 1.5 = ~48 000 calls | very small (memcpy-like) | ~200 µs | absorbed |
| intra prediction | TM_PRED / V / H / DC dominant on keyframes only | ~30 keyframes/sec at most | small | ~negligible | ✓ |
| entropy / bool reader | `BoolReader::read` | ~tokens/frame ~50k+ | ~5-15 ns | ~300-700 µs | absorbed in tile_decode |

**Key observation:** LF is the single largest *unparallelised* µs item AND its kernels are already AVX2. So the lever is NOT "make the LF kernels faster" — it is **"do the same LF work concurrently with something else"** OR **"avoid doing some of the LF work"**.

The Wave 2 attempt did the former (row-MT). That regressed by 7-25% multi-thread because of the post-scope barrier and the per-row `frame_mi` materialisation. The Wave 2 plan §2 explicitly noted the *single-scope* variant (LF jobs in the same scope as tile decode workers) was theoretically better but rejected because `setup_mask` needs `&[ModeInfo]` not `&[Option<ModeInfo>]`. Phase 2 (shared-MI grid) eliminated `grid_stitch` but the LF still materialises `frame_mi` from `Option<ModeInfo>` (`lib.rs:3508-3518`) — this is a leftover serial step.

## 4. Top 3 Wave 5 candidates

Scoring: `impact × confidence / risk`. Impact is % of frame-time on `testsrc_1080p30 t=4` (3 256 µs baseline) that could disappear if the lever lands cleanly.

### #1 — LF prereq decoupling: hoist `frame_mi` materialisation into tile workers + drop the post-decode scope barrier

**Rationale.** The Wave 2 row-MT-LF post-mortem says the *implementation* failed but the underlying analysis (LF is 1.7-3.0 ms serial on main thread) is still true. The W2 plan §2 documented the cleaner "single-scope" topology that *was rejected for an implementation reason* (placeholder fill for `Option<ModeInfo>` cells). The placeholder is just `default_padding_modeinfo` — there is no reason it cannot be written by the *tile workers themselves* as they decode (or by Phase-2's shared-MI grid pre-fill). Then the LF setup_mask loop can run inside the tile-decode scope, overlapped with stragglers, and the post-scope work shrinks to `border` + memcpy. Estimated benefit: shift ~1.0-1.4 ms out of the critical path (the LF setup_mask + mask compute portion; the actual edge-filter work then runs row-MT or stays serial but starts ~1 ms earlier).

**Predicted impact:** **−15 to −25%** on headline (550-800 µs out of 3 256 µs).
**Effort:** medium — touches `lib.rs:3508-3547`, `tile.rs` worker output, and the row-MT LF driver in `apply.rs`. ~200-400 LOC.
**Risk:** medium — must preserve bit-exact conformance; the placeholder-fill semantics must match the existing serial path exactly.
**Confidence:** medium-high. The hypothesis is grounded in the measured 54.7% LF share + the W2 plan §2 root-cause analysis.

### #2 — Inter-tile load imbalance: tile worker join tail

**Rationale.** At `testsrc_1080p30 t=4`, `join = 1131 µs` median, P95 = 3 513 µs (3.1× spread). Sum tile_decode P95 = 13 417 µs / 4 = 3 354 µs/tile worst-case, which closely matches the join P95. This means tile-decode workload imbalance creates a long tail where the last tile holds the whole frame open. On `tile_1x4 t=4` the join median is even worse: 3 498 µs (51% of frame). Two contributors:
  - Tile-column workload is set by the bitstream (encoder choice), so column 3 (rightmost) on synthetic content can be lopsided.
  - Inside a tile, the worker does parse + intra + inter + IDCT + reconstruct serially. No further intra-tile parallelism.

Targeted lever: **switch tile-worker scheduling to work-stealing at the SB-row granularity within a single tile**, OR split the worst tile across two workers. This is a more invasive parallelism shape change than W2 attempted; it requires a real `row_mt` for *decode* (which libvpx implements via the `row_mt` flag for ≥VP9). Wave 5 should size a *measurement-first* sub-task: instrument per-tile work, prove the imbalance is real on multiple fixtures, only then implement.

**Predicted impact:** **−10 to −20%** on multi-thread (200-700 µs from the join tail); larger on `tile_1x4` (~−30% by collapsing the 3.5 ms join).
**Effort:** large — touches the work-distribution + sync primitives in `tile.rs` + `tile_threading/scope.rs`.
**Risk:** high — easy to introduce conformance bugs (the bitstream parse step has tile-internal serial dependencies; an SB-row signal protocol like the W2 C2 work is mandatory and itself hard).
**Confidence:** medium. The data shows the tail; the fix is well-understood (libvpx has a reference impl).

### #3 — convolve8_horiz 256-bit retry on pinned core (the deferred W3)

**Rationale.** W3 was deferred from the morning plan because the previous attempt regressed on Arrow Lake hybrid. The post-W1 share of tile_decode is now smaller (4 348 µs total per frame across 4 cores ≈ 1 087 µs effective per-tile), but inter-prediction MC is still a meaningful slice of that (estimated 800-1 200 µs/frame total; ~25-30% of tile_decode). Faster convolve8_horiz would cut into the parallel decode path AND reduce the join wait. Given the impressive existing horiz_avx2 ratios for vert (~22-26×), widening to 256-bit lanes should yield further 15-30% on horiz which translates to ~5-10% of frame time, *if* the Arrow Lake regression can be fenced off via runtime detection or pinned-core measurement.

**Predicted impact:** **−5 to −10%** on headline (160-330 µs).
**Effort:** medium — port `vpx_convolve8_horiz_avx2` style (the wider variant) + add a hybrid-CPU detect gate.
**Risk:** medium-high — last attempt regressed; needs pinned-core perf gate before merge.
**Confidence:** low-medium. Outcome contingent on Arrow Lake-class hosts not regressing.

### Honourable mentions (NOT in the top 3)

- **`extend_frame_borders` parallelisation:** 85 µs / 2.6%. Real but small. Touch only if it can be folded into a worker already running.
- **`merge` parallelisation:** 85 µs / 2.6%. Same as above. Pooling already took it from 1 246 µs to 85 µs.
- **`setup` further trim:** 85 µs / 2.6%. Looks suspiciously high at P95 = 139 µs — a small win if the per-tile-slot pool's drain/refill is the cost.
- **`BoolReader::read` BMI2 / inline-asm:** The T-3 finding said don't pursue. ns/call measurements were ~5-15 ns; tokens/frame ~50k means ~250-750 µs/frame. That's 8-23% of frame, but the prior analysis said the prob-table-locality wall is the floor; not a viable target.

## 5. What we should NOT pursue, and why

**Row-MT decode pipelining** (the libvpx `row_mt` flag for decode side, i.e. start LF row r as soon as decode row r-1 finishes within a single tile column). The Wave 2 row-MT-LF post-mortem already proved this for the LF stage specifically: at the post-W1+W4 frame size of ~3.3 ms, the `WorkerPool::scope` open/close + atomic gating + per-worker dispatch overhead is genuinely larger than the parallel-work gain from splitting a ~1.8 ms stage across 4 cores. Doing the same thing for the *whole decode pipeline* (parse + reconstruct + LF) would multiply that overhead by N stages and is even less likely to win. The W3-style "wider SIMD on a single hot kernel" and the #1 candidate's "single-scope LF-overlapped-with-decode" attacks are both cheaper *and* better aligned with where the time actually goes. The only context where row-MT pipelining could win is **higher frame sizes (4K) and/or much more expensive bitstreams** (HBD, 4:4:4, very high-Q residuals); at 1080p with our current kernel speeds, the per-row sync tax dominates.
