## Decisions and why

**Sliced by diff, not by subdirectory.** The alternative was a shallow sweep of all 145 files.
A SIMD batch three days old, with no differential-harness coverage, is where a bug is both most
likely and most expensive; a shallow sweep would have found none of what this pass found. The
cost is that `loopfilter/`, `entropy/`, `tile_threading/` and `intra_pred.rs` got no focused
read — recorded in the report's "next pass" section rather than left implicit.

**Verified every agent finding before reporting it.** Two needed correction and one needed
demotion, which is why this matters:

- The unsafe lens was briefed on the premise that `bdb22b494` hardened a *decoder* parser.
  Wrong — I had written the brief from the commit subject, and the commit only touches
  `write_tile_info`, the encoder's writer. The agent caught my error and led with the
  correction. I had independently reached the same conclusion by reading the diff while the
  agents ran, so the two agreed.
- The test-coverage lens rated the `vpx_vector_var_scalar` cfg gap CRITICAL. It isn't: on
  x86_64 the scalar *arm* of `vpx_vector_var` is cfg'd out on the same configuration, so
  nothing calls the missing function and there is no build break. The real cost is test
  integrity. Filed Medium.
- The perf lens asserted libvpx's SSE2 `fdct8x8` applies its overflow check only in HBD builds.
  It couldn't check — `third_party/libvpx/` isn't checked out here — and neither could I, so
  that claim stayed out of the report and out of RVP9-BUG-CRU-00004's body.

**Derived F2 by hand rather than trusting a hunch.** The test-coverage lens said the
`pass1_is_safe` fallback branch "may or may not" be hit by the extreme tests and "can't be
confirmed by reading". It can. For a constant residual of magnitude `v`, the first-pass DC
output is `round(32v · COSPI_16_64 / 2^14) = round(22.62·v)`, so the `[-4096, 4095]` guard fires
for `|v| > 181`. I worked all four extreme groups through the butterfly by hand: constant ±255
gives 5771, axis-0 gives `output[7] = -5228`, axis-1 gives 5771, impulse gives 5770. All four
fall back. That turns "possible gap" into "these assertions compare the scalar oracle with
itself and cannot fail", which is a filable bug rather than a hunch. The perf lens reached the
same conclusion independently for the constant case.

## The surprise

I went in expecting to find a bit-exactness bug in the intrinsics. There isn't one. Every
bounds proof holds, every rounding formula matches, and `pass1_is_safe`'s `[-4096, 4095]` bound
is *exactly* tight — `8 × 4095 = 32760` fits i16 with 7 to spare, and a symmetric ±4096 bound
would wrap at `8 × 4096 = 32768`. Someone derived that carefully.

What the batch actually lost is verification, in three independent places, all invisible from
the diff:

1. Six kernel families moved from the runtime `SimdDispatch` table to bare compile-time
   `#[cfg(target_arch)]`, so `RVP9_FORCE_SCALAR` can no longer reach them. Both legs of the
   documented byte-identity oracle now run the same SSE2 code. Before this batch x86_64 ran
   scalar for all six, so the variable was effectively honoured; the gap existed on AArch64
   already and this batch made it live on the platform the release gates target.
2. The 8x8 extreme tests are vacuous (F2).
3. The integral-projection scalar oracle stopped compiling on x86_64, so its SSE2 kernel is
   checked against a hand-rewritten copy in the test module.

Each looks locally reasonable. Together they mean the newest, least-covered kernels are the
least-checked ones — the opposite of what you want. Worth remembering that "is this still
*checked*?" is a distinct question from "is this correct?", and a perf commit is exactly where
the answer quietly changes.

Compounding it: the test-coverage lens grepped `crates/rvp9-harness/` for every symbol in the
diff and found zero matches. None of the five kernels is reachable from any checked-in
differential or conformance tool. Conformance is decode-side and these are all encoder-side, so
a wrong forward transform still produces a valid, decodable, merely worse bitstream. The in-file
unit tests are the entire net.

## Dead end

Chased whether the fused 64x64 integral-projection kernel diverges from the scalar path it
replaces, expecting a normalisation mismatch (`/ (height>>1)` vs `>> 5`, and the
`norm_factor = 3 + (bw>>5)` column path). Traced every offset and shift by hand: `ref_h` covers
rows `sb_px_y..+64` / columns `base_x_row..+128`, `ref_v` covers rows `base_y_col..+128` /
columns `sb_px_x..+64` at shift 5, `src_h`/`src_v` likewise. They agree exactly, and `/32` and
`>>5` coincide because the sums are sums of unsigned bytes. No bug. Kept the finding anyway, at
Low, because nothing *pins* the two paths together and they're selected by a runtime
interior-vs-edge condition (F7).

Also chased the `write_literal(bits > 32)` path as a sibling of the `write_tile_info` fix.
Refuted: documented, clamped, and buffer overflow sets a poison flag. The two real siblings are
`write_loopfilter` and `write_delta_q` (F5).
