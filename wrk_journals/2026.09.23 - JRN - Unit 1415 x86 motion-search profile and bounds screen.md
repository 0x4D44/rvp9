# Unit 1415 — x86 motion-search profile and rejected bounds screen

Fresh exact Crucible profiling of integrated `5e8426ac` used 20 serial
1080p Ducks encodes. All outputs matched. The four-reference SAD helper has
1,503,616 weighted self samples, about 3.3% of encoder symbol weight; its
diamond-search caller has 1,998,136. The trace lost three events, and a
bounded IP-to-line extraction did not finish. The build, artifacts, hashes,
and protocol are recorded in
`wrk_docs/evidence/2026-09-23-unit1415-profile/README.md`.

I re-screened the earlier Unit 1287 idea under the current Tesco retention
rule: one checked union envelope followed by direct slices, retaining the
scalar fallback for clipped or malformed references. The focused differential
failed deliberately on a wrong lane value, then passed; AArch64 core tests,
x86 motion-search tests, formatting, and targeted clippy passed. Both Crucible
screens were output-exact. The first paired corpus ratio was 1.019292; the
build-flags-matched repeat was 1.023270, with every clip slower. Reject the
candidate. Its exact patch is archived beside the raw screens, and the source
was restored to the integrated blob before the evidence commit.

Fresh direct comparison of that integrated control with pinned libvpx
`12f3a2ac` gave a 1.230937 paired corpus ratio over the same three clips,
three pairs, and two rotations. Asymmetric multi-second stalls in both arms
limit precision, but the result agrees with Unit 1410's 1.227734 much better
than Unit 1414's noisy 1.433202. The 1.05 target remains unmet. At this
ratio, roughly 14.7% less RVP9 runtime would be needed to reach 1.05 if the
reference time held fixed; this is an illustrative arithmetic gap, not a
forecast for any one optimization.

The benchmark harness samples Windows CPU for 60 seconds at every clip even
when no load ceiling is requested. A four-rotation three-clip screen therefore
spends about 14 minutes just on load checks; this was recorded separately as
`RVP9-BUG-FLU-00033`, not fixed during the codec task.
