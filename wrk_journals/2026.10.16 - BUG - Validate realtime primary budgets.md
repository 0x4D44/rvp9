# Validate realtime primary budgets — KIL-00113

The realtime benchmark parsed its primary latency budget as an unrestricted `f64`. Positive infinity therefore made every finite average satisfy the gate, while the p99 budget already required a finite positive value.

The correction applies the same finite-positive assertion and diagnostic immediately after primary budget parsing. Existing parser style, defaults, accepted finite positive budgets, timing-gate calculations and output artifact behavior remain otherwise unchanged.

Without the guard, the selected rejection test ran once and failed `--budget-ms must reject NaN in split form` (ten filtered out). A separate preservation-control mutation added an artificial `<= 1_000_000.0` bound; its selected test failed on the valid `f64::MAX` budget at the parser assertion. Removing the artificial bound restored the intended finite-and-positive condition.

All 11 realtime unit tests passed after restoration, including the existing bounded fake-child check. The lead independently reran all 11. Invalid-budget coverage checks NaN, both infinities, zero, negative zero and a negative value in split and inline forms, including the exact validation diagnostic. Positive controls preserve the default, the smallest subnormal and smallest normal positive values, ordinary budgets and the largest finite value. Scoped realtime Clippy passed with warnings denied, as did workspace formatting and diff checks. Tests used the task's absolute `.scratch` as `TMPDIR`; no real encoder, clip or performance measurement was used.

The preselected final smoke after catch-up is `cargo test -p rvp9-bench --bin realtime tests::primary_budget_requires_finite_positive_value -- --exact`, bounded to 60 seconds.
