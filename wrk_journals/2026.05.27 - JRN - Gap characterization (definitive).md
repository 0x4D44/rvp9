# JRN — Definitive gap characterization (team-lead)

**Date:** 2026-05-27
**Author:** team-lead
**Status:** capstone for the 2026-05-26/27 "match vpxdec" drive.

## The bottom line

After rigorous PGO/in-situ measurement, the headline gap (`testsrc_1080p30
t=4` ≈ 4.79× slower than vpxdec) decomposes into **two structural causes,
neither a quick lever**:

1. **~29-37% worker idle at t=4** (parallelism / fork-join inefficiency).
   Measured on the PGO build: worker threads spend 28.7% in `ntdll`
   (NtWaitForAlertByThreadId parking) + ~9% kernel (sync/schedule). PGO
   can't touch this — it's scheduling, not codegen. Reclaiming it needs
   **within-tile row-MT decode** (libvpx `row_mt=1` port, ~400-700 LOC,
   architectural). RISKY: the prior Wave-2 row-MT-LF attempt was 1.5×
   SLOWER (row-barrier sync overhead > idle reclaimed). I previously
   dismissed parallelism on flawed non-PGO t=1≈t=4 ratio logic — that was
   wrong; the idle is real and unexhausted.

2. **~2-3× per-work overhead when busy** (diffuse). Even at full
   utilization we'd be ~2-3× slower than vpxdec per frame-of-work. NOT a
   single lever — spread across the reconstruct driver, abstraction
   (TileFrameView, per-block setup), memory/cache behavior, and kernels
   that are ~1.6× (not 10×) slower in-situ.

## What we PROVED (the disproven levers)

Four structural/kernel levers tested under proper PGO/in-situ measurement,
all evaporated — every one looked big in non-PGO/isolated benches and
collapsed in the shipped hot loop:

| Lever | non-PGO / isolated | PGO / in-situ | verdict |
|-------|--------------------|---------------|---------|
| Inline dispatch (Lever A) | +6% | ≈0 (±5% noise) | PGO devirtualizes dispatch |
| Zero-copy `above` (Lever B) | ~8ns/block | ≈0 | OOO core hides L1-hot 4-8B copy |
| Intra envelope collapse | 10-25× kernel gap | **−3-4% REGRESSION** | the 10-25× was a COLD-PATH microbench artifact; in-hot-loop the wrapper is already 0.77-1.55ns ≈ libvpx's 0.6ns. Forcing the umbrella added i-cache pressure. |
| Entropy testsrc half | −10% | ≈0 | testsrc win was indirection PGO handles |

**The durable principle (earned the hard way ×4):** non-PGO and isolated
microbenchmarks systematically OVERSTATE gaps, because they expose call
envelopes / indirection / cold-path costs that PGO + the warm out-of-order
core erase in the real decode loop. Only PGO end-to-end A/B is trustworthy.
**Corollary the intra-kernel head-to-head got wrong:** "our kernels are
10-25× slower" was a cold-path artifact; in-situ they're ~1.6× — near
parity. The kernel-audit's convolve8 2.37× / idct32x32 1.68× are likely
more real (they're heavier kernels measured closer to in-situ), but even
those want PGO end-to-end confirmation before a campaign.

## The one durable win

**Entropy fused single-pass dequant** (`4afe855`, merged): bit-exact,
−97 LOC, **−5% on tile_1x4 PGO** (real work removal — survives PGO). The
only structural lever that held. testsrc half was a mirage.

## Honest conclusion on the 2× target

On intra-heavy 1080p gate fixtures, **2× is not reachable via quick/
structural levers** — those are exhausted (proven). The remaining paths are
both major and risky:

- **(A) Within-tile row-MT decode** — attacks the ~29% idle. ~400-700 LOC,
  architectural, prior row-MT regressed. Could yield up to ~1.4× on t=4 IF
  sync overhead stays below the idle reclaimed. Needs careful column-
  wavefront design (cf. the Lever-B-v2 LF work that DID land clean).
- **(B) Diffuse per-work reduction** — attacks the ~2-3× busy overhead.
  Needs microarchitectural profiling (VTune — NOT installed; needs an
  interactive 1-2GB install + admin driver) to localize cache/branch/
  frontend bottlenecks, since sampling can't decompose the PGO inline blob
  and the kernels are ~at parity. Likely many small fixes, not one lever.

Both warrant a strategic go/no-go (multi-week, risky) — this is the
"discuss architectural decisions before implementing" line. Quick-lever
autonomy is spent; spinning more would be value-negative (4 disproven).
