# Reject singular BD-rate fits — KIL-00107

The benchmark's cubic solver returned four zero coefficients when its normal equations were singular. The caller treated those coefficients as a fit; two undefined curves could therefore yield a finite 0% BD-rate. Merely counting four observations does not establish four independent PSNR values.

The bounded correction keeps the existing normal-equation algorithm, makes fit failure explicit, and rejects fewer than four distinct PSNR inputs before numerical elimination. Failure propagates through `bd_rate` and `measured_bd_rate`; the existing main path returns failure before the optional CSV writer. Repeated observations remain valid when at least four distinct PSNR values support the cubic. The separately tracked FPS-reporting defect KIL-00116 is outside this fix.

The solver returns `None` for non-finite input, singular/non-finite pivots, or non-finite coefficients. `measured_bd_rate` reports an explicit insufficient/singular/non-overlapping-points error. The normal-equation arithmetic, integration, CSV format, and runtime artifact handling otherwise retain their existing contracts.

Observed regression proofs:

- Before the fix, singular anchor, singular test, and two singular curves with overlapping PSNR `[0, 1, 1, 2]` each failed the expected rejection assertion.
- Before the fix, a singular anchor at realistic PSNR `[28, 34, 34, 40]` also failed its rejection assertion.
- Removing only the explicit distinct-PSNR guard after making the solver fallible again failed the realistic singular assertion: pivot roundoff still produced a finite result. The guard was restored manually.
- Deliberately rejecting every duplicate PSNR failed the full-rank repeated-observation acceptance test at its successful-result assertion. The intended guard was restored manually.

The independent analytical controls use identical curves (0%) and a 1.25 bitrate multiplier (25%) at `[28, 34, 34, 40, 46, 46]`. Both passed on the original and corrected algorithms.
