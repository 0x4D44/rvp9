## The earned, durable principle

**Non-PGO and isolated microbenchmarks systematically overstate gaps.**
The warm out-of-order core + PGO's indirect-call promotion + i-cache
locality erase call-envelope and indirection costs that cold-path benches
loudly advertise. Five times, four wrong. The infamous "intra kernels are
10-25× slower than libvpx" was a cold-call mirage — in the real hot loop
our predictors run at ~1ns ≈ libvpx's 0.6ns.

**Only PGO end-to-end A/B with interleaved drift-cancelling iterations on
the actual gate fixture is trustworthy.** This is now baked into every
sign-off condition for future work.

## UPDATE — 6th investigation, closing diagnostic (overlap lever class CLOSED)

After 5, I did one more: a focused diagnostic ("did the overlap miss for
fixable reasons or fundamental?"). Agent verdict: **(B) FUNDAMENTAL** with a
specific mechanistic explanation — the impl actually USED the
HLD-recommended row-dispatch strategy correctly; the miss was the **pool
topology**, not the row-claim logic. With 4 workers + 4 decode jobs running
whole-tile critical paths on a single FIFO channel, LF jobs are
scheduling-starved during decode regardless of how cleverly they claim
rows. The "overlap window" equals the tile imbalance (~2.6% ≈ 30µs); the
HLD's `(decode_work + LF_work)/4` model required both job classes
simultaneously across 4 lanes, which **requires either per-row decode jobs
(within-tile row-MT, the just-shelved 400-700 LOC option) or more OS
threads than cores** (libvpx uses a separate `lf_worker` thread set in
addition to the decode pool — different architecture).

A clean-room overlap redo on the current decode-job topology would miss
again. The overlap *lever class* is closed.
