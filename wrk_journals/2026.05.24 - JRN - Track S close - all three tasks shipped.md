## Task B Phase 1 — `BoolReader::fill` hot/cold split

`bddcc64`, 1 file, +46/-22 LOC.

Refactor:
- `fill()` `#[inline]`-hinted, ~9-line fast path (8-byte `from_be_bytes`
  load + shift + OR). Now small enough that LLVM may inline at
  `read()` call sites without icache bloat.
- `fill_slow()` `#[cold] #[inline(never)]`, ~16-line EOF byte-loop.
  Out-of-line. Body byte-for-byte identical to the previous slow
  path; `bits_left` and `shift` are passed in to avoid recomputation.

`#[cold]` alone is a hint; `#[inline(never)]` is what actually keeps
LLVM from re-merging the body back into `fill`. Both required.

Bit-exact preserved: 201/201 conformance, all four harness gates pass.

Bench (Task C, 5+5 sweep, ts 1779589201 vs 1779590057):
- **All fixtures NOISE.** No statistically-significant wins or
  regressions at α=0.05, |Δ|≥5%, n=5.
- 5 of 9 fixtures trend better (-1.6% to -6.2%); 4 trend worse
  (+1.0% to +5.6%).
- Worst trend: `tile_1x4 t=4` +5.6% at p=0.15 (NOISE) — could be a
  real small regression at n=5 power limit, or could be noise on
  the same fixture that won big with Task A.

Decision: **keep**. The architectural improvement is real (hot/cold
split mirrors libvpx, addresses R.15's documented failure mode
explicitly). No statistically-significant regression. Zero ongoing
maintenance cost. The bench can't statistically confirm a perf win
at this power level, but the change is correct code structure.

## What Phase 2 of Task B (BMI2) would look like

The brief mentioned BMI2 `BLSR` / `PEXT` as a "possibly" hand-write to
replace LZCNT + shift in the renormalization sequence. I did NOT
implement Phase 2 because:

- The brief itself hedged with "Possibly".
- The required instruction sequence isn't obvious — LZCNT (which we
  already have from R.7) is the canonical renormalization tool;
  BLSR / PEXT don't have an obvious slot in arithmetic-coder fast
  paths.
- The expected win from Phase 1 alone (15-30% per the scoping report)
  was already small enough that the bench framework couldn't
  statistically confirm it at n=5. Phase 2's additional 15-20% would
  need n=20+ to detect reliably given the 13-21% noise floor.
- The bench framework (Task C) is now in place so a future Phase 2
  attempt can be evaluated honestly.

If perf is the gate for shipping, the next investment with positive
expected value is the assembly-grade bitreader work (3 weeks per the
original brief), not BMI2 intrinsics.
