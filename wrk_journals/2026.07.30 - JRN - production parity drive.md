## Unit 84 — correct the Stage 4a thread-count baseline

The pinned `vpxenc` profile showed four OS threads even though the prior
comparison omitted `--threads`. The CLI's VP9 default requests the maximum
legal number of tile columns (`tile_columns = 6`, clamped by frame width);
at 1280 pixels that is four column tiles. In contrast, rvp9 parses
`--threads` and `--tile-columns` but does not copy either value into
`EncoderConfig`; its frame loop always emits and encodes one tile.

Fresh warmed ten-encode aggregates used identical explicit thread counts,
120 frames of `testsrc_1280x704_120f.y4m`, one-pass VBR, 2 Mbps,
`--rt --cpu-used=7 --lag-in-frames=0 --aq-mode=0`:

- one thread: rvp9 `15.58 s`, vpxenc `3.73 s`, ratio `4.18x`;
- four threads: rvp9 `15.57 s`, vpxenc `1.71 s`, ratio `9.11x`.

The rvp9 `--threads=4` result is identical to `--threads=1`, proving the
flag is currently inert. Pinned vpxenc's default output is byte-identical
to explicit `--tile-columns=2`; explicit log2 tile columns `0`, `1`, and
`2` produce distinct streams. Pinned vpxenc four-thread `--row-mt=0`
matches its default stream and time (`1.70 s` for ten encodes);
`--row-mt=1` produces a distinct stream and measured `1.63 s`. Therefore
the relevant canonical speed mechanism is four independent column tiles,
not row-mt.

The corrected measurement invalidates the earlier 9–11x single-thread
interpretation but exposes the more important product defect: rvp9 cannot
meet the required same-thread-count gate until encoder tile columns and
tile workers are real. The next unit is architecture, not another scalar
leaf optimization: wire explicit tile/thread configuration, make tile
boundaries semantically correct, then parallelize independent column
tiles while keeping a one-thread/single-tile oracle.
