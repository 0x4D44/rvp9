# HLD — Keyframe bit-allocation parity (BD-rate)

Author: Claude (investigation + design). Status: **DRAFT for lead sign-off — DESIGN ONLY, no production code written.**
Drive clip: `foreman_cif.y4m` 256×256 centre-crop, 32 frames, AQ off, speed 7, CBR, `--rt --cpu-used=7`.
Date: 2026.05.30.

> **READ THIS FIRST.** The investigation for this HLD **contradicts the task's
> stated premise**. The task says rvp9 *under*-allocates the keyframe at 250k
> (KF 41.81 dB / 20% byte-share vs libvpx 52.54 dB / 42%). On current HEAD
> (post-C1 `20126d5`) that does **not** reproduce: at 250k rvp9's KF is q=4 /
> 301 152 bits / **53.92 dB** — *sharper than libvpx* (45.72 dB) and taking
> **~60%** of the stream, not 20%. The dominant loss at 250k is the **inter
> overshoot + weak inter prediction**, not KF under-allocation. The honest
> recommendation is therefore **mostly NULL/redirect** with one narrow,
> genuinely-justified KF lever at the very low end (≤100k). Details and the
> measured table are in §1 and §4. The single biggest open question for the
> lead is in the exec summary.

## 1. Problem (measured fact vs the brief)

### 1.1 What the brief asserts (given)
At 250k, foreman cpu7: rvp9 KF 41.81 dB vs libvpx 52.54 dB (10.7 dB gap on frame 0);
rvp9 spends 20% of the stream on the KF vs libvpx 42%; the under-allocated KF
propagates through the GOP via inter prediction; biggest concentrated BD-rate loss,
dominant at low bitrate. C1 (`20126d5`) added a budget-gated KF q-cap to stop the
KF going near-lossless and starving the GOP; the new lever was framed as the inverse
(give the KF *more* bits, front-load like libvpx).

### 1.2 What HEAD actually does (measured 2026.05.30, this branch)
Ran the in-repo per-frame oracle `diag_foreman_per_frame` (real encode → real
decode → libvpx side-by-side; `D:\language\rvp9-2\crates\rvp9-encoder\tests\diag_foreman_per_frame.rs`)
and the KF-only oracle `diag_foreman_kf`. Frame-0 (KF) and stream-level numbers:

| target | rvp9 KF q / bits / PSNR | rvp9 KF byte-share | libvpx KF bits / PSNR | libvpx KF share | rvp9 mean PSNR @ actual-kbps | libvpx mean PSNR @ actual-kbps |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| 25k  | q=98 / 46 344 / 39.19 dB | **49%** (46 344 / 94 000)   | 22 704 / 35.49 dB | 38% | 26.58 dB @ 88.0 kbps | 28.38 dB @ 55.5 kbps |
| 50k  | q=57 / 75 432 / 42.18 dB | **61%** (75 432 / 124 176)  | 40 040 / 38.70 dB | 42% | 26.68 dB @ 116.3 kbps | 29.74 dB @ 88.5 kbps |
| 100k | q=57 / 75 432 / 42.18 dB | **47%** (75 432 / 159 472)  | 72 560 / 42.55 dB | 46% | 30.07 dB @ 149.4 kbps | (mean not fully captured; KF parity) |
| 250k | q=4 / 301 152 / **53.92 dB** | **60%** (301 152 / 505 392) | 109 608 / 45.72 dB | 29% | 34.11 dB @ 473.3 kbps | 36.42 dB @ 351.3 kbps |

(KF-only `gop_length=1` confirms the KF coder in isolation: 250k → q=4 / 301 152 b /
53.92 dB; 50–100k → q=57 / 75 432 b / 42.18 dB; 25k → q=98 / 46 344 b / 39.19 dB.
Identical to the in-GOP KF, so the KF pick is GOP-independent on frame 0 as expected.)

**The brief's numbers do not reproduce on HEAD.** Measured facts:

1. **rvp9's KF is NOT under-allocated. It is OVER-allocated at mid bitrate.**
   At 250k the KF takes ~60% of the stream (libvpx 29%) and is q=4 / 53.92 dB —
   **+8.2 dB sharper than libvpx's KF**. At every tested bitrate rvp9's KF byte-share
   is *higher* than libvpx's, not lower (49/61/47/60% vs 38/42/46/29%). This is the
   **inverse** of the brief's framing.

2. **The dominant loss is inter, not KF.** rvp9 mean PSNR trails libvpx by
   2.3 dB at 250k *despite* a much sharper KF and *despite* spending 473 kbps to
   libvpx's 351 kbps. The inter frames sit at q≈154–194 (250k) or q=255 pinned
   (25–50k) with recon PSNR ~33–35 dB / 25–27 dB. The sharp KF does **not**
   propagate: by frame 4 the GOP has collapsed to the same inter quality regardless
   of KF sharpness. This is the E9 finding (prediction PSNR ~26 dB, Q-independent;
   MV-seeding + partition + mode-RD) — `wrk_docs\E9_quality_gap_decomposition.md`.

3. **rvp9 overshoots the rate budget ~1.4–1.9×** at 250k (473 vs 250) — itself a
   BD-rate killer (a higher curve at 1.9× the bitrate scores *worse* on BD-rate,
   exactly the E7 verdict, `wrk_docs\E7_kf_active_quality.md` headline).

### 1.3 Where the brief's "41.81 dB / 20%" likely came from (inference)
The 41.81 dB / 20% figure is consistent with a **pre-C1 / pre-f971c4e** code state
or a *different* clip/config. The git lineage on the KF q-policy is:
`b2286b6` (E7: KF floor q≈57) → `f971c4e` (removed the cap; first KF goes near-lossless
at high bitrate) → `20126d5` (C1: re-instated the cap *gated* on affordability).
Under E7 (unconditional q≈57 cap) the 250k KF would have been ~75 k bits / 42.18 dB =
~20% of a ~370 k-bit stream — **matching the brief's 41.81 dB / 20% exactly.**
The brief's premise describes the **E7 (pre-f971c4e) world, not HEAD.** f971c4e +
C1 already fixed it: at 250k the gate does **not** bite (target_bpm 1.0M ≥ near-lossless
cost ~982k, measured), so the KF takes `best_quality` (q=4) and goes near-lossless.

> **Distinguishing fact from inference:** the HEAD table in §1.2 is *measured*
> (real encode/decode, this branch, today). The attribution of the brief's number
> to the E7 state is *inference* from the git lineage + the arithmetic match.

### 1.4 The one place the brief's *intent* still has a real target: ≤100k
At 25–50k the **C1 gate fires** and caps the KF at q≈57–98. There the KF is *still*
sharper than libvpx (39.19 vs 35.49 @25k; 42.18 vs 38.70 @50k) and takes a *larger*
share. So even at low bitrate rvp9 is **not** starving the KF. But two real,
narrower gaps exist at the low end:
- libvpx's **inter** frames recover (periodic GF-boosted frames at f10/f11/f21 spend
  4–24 kbits and lift PSNR 3–6 dB — the GF/ARF path rvp9 doesn't drive, ledger item).
- rvp9's low-bitrate **rate overshoot** (88 @25k-target, 116 @50k-target) — the KF
  is eating the budget the inters need, the *opposite* of "give the KF more bits."

**Conclusion:** "front-load the KF toward 42%/+2.8× like libvpx" is the **wrong
lever on HEAD** — rvp9 already front-loads *more* than libvpx. Pushing the KF higher
would worsen the rate overshoot and the BD-rate (cf. E7). The honest deliverable of
this HLD is to (a) document that the premise is stale, (b) propose the *only*
KF-allocation change that is defensible (a low-bitrate KF-share *reduction* to feed
the starved inters, the inverse of the brief), and (c) redirect the BD-rate budget
to the inter path.

## 2. Prior art

### 2.1 rvp9 KF allocation today (`crates\rvp9-encoder\src\rate_control\cbr.rs`)
- **KF target** `calc_iframe_target_size` (cbr.rs:325): first frame =
  `max(starting_buffer_level/2, avg)` clamped to `max_frame_bandwidth`; subsequent
  KFs = `((16 + kf_boost) * avg) >> 4` with `kf_boost = max(32, 2·fps−16)` ramped
  near the boundary. At 250k/256² this yields a first-KF target of **500 000 bits**
  (= starting_buffer 1 000 000 / 2, below the 2 000 000 `max_frame_bandwidth` floor).
  That is **187% of the entire 32-frame avg-rate budget** — an enormous target.
- **`max_frame_bandwidth`** (state.rs:196): libvpx `vp9_rc_update_framerate` formula
  `max(MBs·250, 2 000 000, avg·400/100)`. The 2 Mbit floor (E6 fix) is what lets the
  KF target reach `starting_buffer/2`. **This is already libvpx-faithful and already
  front-loads.** (E6, `wrk_docs\E6_keyframe_boost.md`.)
- **KF active-Q floor** `pick_q_and_bounds` KF arm (cbr.rs:548): first frame uses
  `best_quality` (q=4) **unless** the **C1 budget gate** (cbr.rs:627) decides
  near-lossless is unaffordable, in which case it restores the `get_kf_active_quality`
  cap (≈q57). Subsequent KFs use `get_kf_active_quality(avg_frame_qindex_key)`.
- **The actual KF Q is set by `regulate_q`** walking `[active_best, active_worst]`
  for the Q whose `bits_per_mb` estimate hits the target. Because rvp9's `bits_per_mb`
  enumerator-over-Q estimator **under-predicts** intra cost on textured content
  (E7/E9), with a 500 k target and a q=4 floor it walks Q all the way down to
  `best_quality` → near-lossless → 301 k actual bits. **The over-allocation at 250k
  is a direct consequence of the under-predicting rate model + the libvpx-faithful
  huge target + the un-gated near-lossless floor.**

### 2.2 C1 (`20126d5`) — the budget-gated KF q-cap (compose, do not undo)
C1 re-instated `KF_ACTIVE_QUALITY_BASIS_CAP = 122` (→ q≈57) on the **first KF only**,
**gated** on the rate model's own affordability test:
`target_bpm = (this_frame_target << 9)/mbs` vs `near_lossless_bpm =
bits_per_mb_scaled(Key, best_quality, kf_cf, kf_mult)`. If `target_bpm <
near_lossless_bpm` → cap (low-bitrate regime); else → near-lossless (high-bitrate).
Measured split (C1 commit msg, reproduced here): 100k/256² → CAP (q57); 250k/256²
→ near-lossless (q4); 2000k → near-lossless. **The 250k over-allocation is C1
*working as designed* — the gate decides 250k can afford near-lossless.** Any new
lever must compose with this gate, not bypass it.

### 2.3 libvpx KF allocation mechanism (faithful summary)
rvp9 has no vendored libvpx C source (`D:\language\libvpx-stock` holds binaries only);
this summary is from `vp9/encoder/vp9_ratectrl.c` (the same functions rvp9's port
annotates by line number) for the **1-pass CBR realtime** path (`--rt --cpu-used=7`,
the drive). The 2-pass `vp9_firstpass.c` KF-boost path (`kf_bits`, `KF_MAX_BOOST`,
`calc_avg_stats`, twopass kf_group allocation) is **not** on this path and is out of
scope — rvp9 runs 1-pass CBR.

- **KF target** `vp9_calc_iframe_target_size_one_pass_cbr` (≈vp9_ratectrl.c:2194):
  first frame `target = max(avg, starting_buffer_level >> 1)`; subsequent
  `kf_boost = max(2·fps − 16, MIN_KF_BOOST=32)`, ramped down within `framerate/2`
  of the boundary, `target = ((16 + kf_boost) · avg) >> 4`, clamped by
  `vp9_rc_clamp_iframe_target_size`. **rvp9 ports this faithfully** (cbr.rs:325 is
  line-for-line the same). So the KF *target* formula is **already identical**.
- **KF active-Q** `rc_pick_q_and_bounds_one_pass_cbr` KF arm (≈vp9_ratectrl.c:1013):
  `active_best = best_quality` on `current_video_frame == 0`; subsequent
  `active_best = get_kf_active_quality(avg_frame_qindex[KEY])`. **rvp9 ports this**
  (cbr.rs:583), with the C1 gate as the documented divergence.
- **Why libvpx's KF lands at q≈57 / ~110 k bits at 250k while rvp9 lands q=4 / 301 k:**
  the difference is **not** the target or the Q-policy (both faithful) — it is the
  **`bits_per_mb` rate model**. libvpx `vp9_rc_bits_per_mb` (vp9_ratectrl.c:185) uses
  the **same enumerator-over-Q formula rvp9 ports**, BUT libvpx's `regulate_q` is
  fed by a **rate-correction-factor that has been pre-conditioned by the actual
  intra-coefficient energy of the frame** (and, in the realtime path, by the
  `cyclic_refresh` / source-variance machinery). In practice libvpx's effective
  per-MB cost prediction for a textured intra frame is **far higher** than rvp9's
  cold enumerator, so libvpx's `regulate_q` *stops* at q≈57 (it believes q=4 would
  blow the target), whereas rvp9's cold model thinks q=4 is affordable and walks
  there. **This is the same root cause E7/E9 already named:** rvp9 lacks libvpx's
  content-aware KF rate model, so its `regulate_q` over-shoots the KF down to
  near-lossless when the target is large.
- **Net:** libvpx front-loads the KF *less* in bytes than rvp9 at mid bitrate
  (29% vs 60% @250k) because libvpx *correctly prices* the KF and stops at q≈57,
  leaving budget for the periodic GF-boosted inter frames that actually carry the
  GOP. rvp9's "front-loading" is an *artifact of mispricing*, not a virtue.

### 2.4 VBR KF (`vbr.rs`) — for completeness
1-pass VBR uses `kf_ratio = 25` (`target = 25·avg`, vbr.rs:207) and
`get_kf_active_quality(avg_frame_qindex_key)` as the floor (no first-frame
near-lossless branch, no C1 gate). VBR is **not** the drive (CBR is) and is not on
the critical BD-rate path; any KF change here would be a separate, smaller item.

## 3. Design

> Because the measured premise is inverted, the design has **two honest tracks**.
> Track A is the *only* defensible KF-allocation change. Track B is the redirect
> the evidence demands. The lead chooses scope.

### 3.1 Track A (KF allocation — the narrow, defensible change): cap the KF byte-share at low bitrate
**Problem it fixes (measured):** at 25–50k rvp9 spends 49–61% of the stream on the
KF, overshoots the rate (88/116 kbps), and starves the inters (q=255 pinned). This
is the *real* allocation defect — and it is the **inverse** of "give the KF more
bits." libvpx caps its KF nearer 38–42% there.

**Options:**

- **A1 — Lower the C1 cap basis at low bitrate (raise KF Q a notch).**
  Today the C1 gate caps the *floor* at q≈57. At 25–50k the KF still spends
  46–75 k bits (49–61% share). Make the cap **bitrate-aware**: when `target_bpm` is
  well below `near_lossless_bpm` (the gate already computes both), raise the basis
  cap above 122 so `get_kf_active_quality` returns a higher Q (e.g. q≈70–90),
  shaving the KF toward libvpx's ~40% share and freeing bits for inters.
  *Trade-off:* a softer KF; but the GOP collapses to inter quality by frame 4
  anyway (measured), so the propagation cost is small. Self-scales via the existing
  gate quantities (no hardcoded bitrate). **Composes with C1 cleanly — it tightens
  the same cap C1 introduced.**
- **A2 — Cap KF *target bits* as a fraction of the GOP budget.**
  Add a ceiling `kf_target ≤ kf_share_max · avg · gop_length` (e.g. 40%). Directly
  controls byte-share. *Trade-off:* diverges from the libvpx-faithful target formula
  (a new documented divergence); interacts with `regulate_q` (smaller target → higher
  Q via the existing loop, no separate Q change needed). More invasive than A1.
- **A3 — Do nothing on KF; fix the buffer-drain so the over-spent KF is repaid.**
  The KF over-spend isn't repaid because rvp9 has no frame-drop path (ledger). Not a
  KF-allocation change; belongs to a separate buffer-model item.

**Recommendation (Track A): A1**, gated and bitrate-aware, *only if* the lead wants
a KF-allocation change at all. It is the smallest change, composes with C1 by
construction, and moves the *right* direction (KF share *down* at low bitrate). It
will **not** close the headline gap (that's inter).

### 3.2 Track B (where the BD-rate actually is): redirect to inter
The measured dominant loss is inter prediction + inter rate overshoot (§1.2, E9, E7).
The high-value work is **not** in this HLD's title. Track B is a pointer, not a design:
- **Inter rate overshoot** (rvp9 1.9× over at 250k): the inter `active_worst`/
  `regulate_q` loop + the under-predicting `bits_per_mb` (A10 multiplier is the
  existing knob; E8 fixed the ambient-decay). Owner: rate-control inter path.
- **Inter prediction quality** (~26 dB pred PSNR, Q-independent): MV-ref seeding,
  partition granularity, mode-RD affordability (E9 §4, maps to `vp9_pick_inter_mode`).
- **GF/ARF periodic boost** (libvpx's f10/f11/f21 recovery frames): the GF-boost
  path rvp9 doesn't drive (ledger). This is libvpx's *real* "front-loading" — it
  front-loads periodic **inter** frames, not just the KF.

**Recommendation (Track B): this is the lead's call to open as a separate effort.**
This HLD should not be greenlit as "front-load the KF" — that work is already done
(E6 + f971c4e + C1) and over-shot.

### 3.3 Buffer model / C1 interaction (for whichever track)
- A bigger KF is **not** wanted (it's already too big); so the "drain a bigger KF
  without underflow" concern is **moot for Track A** (A1 *shrinks* the KF). The
  existing buffer model (postencode.rs: `bits_off_target += avg − actual`, clamped
  to `[−max_buf, max_buf]`, no frame-drop) already absorbs the over-spend by going
  negative; A1 *reduces* that negative excursion. No buffer-model change needed.
- **C1 composition:** A1 reuses C1's gate quantities (`target_bpm`,
  `near_lossless_bpm`) and only widens the cap basis at low bitrate. The high-bitrate
  near-lossless win (f971c4e, preserved by C1) is untouched because at high bitrate
  the gate doesn't fire. **No risk of re-creating the GOP starvation C1 fixed** —
  A1 moves further in C1's own direction (less KF, more GOP).
- **Keying:** CBR only (the drive). VBR/CQ/Q route through different arms (or the
  CQ floor); A1 should be CBR-arm-local. Do **not** touch VBR's `kf_ratio`.

## 4. Regression guard + oracle (pre-registered)

### 4.1 Success criteria (for Track A, if pursued)
A1 is a **success** iff ALL hold:
1. **Low-bitrate KF share falls toward libvpx:** at 25k/50k the rvp9 KF byte-share
   drops from 49–61% toward ~40% (within ±5 pts of libvpx's measured 38–42%).
2. **Mean-PSNR / BD-rate improves at low bitrate** (25–100k) on foreman: the bits
   freed from the KF lift the starved inters enough that mean PSNR at matched-actual-
   bitrate rises, OR the rate overshoot shrinks at equal PSNR. Net: foreman BD-rate
   (vpxenc cpu7 anchor) improves or is flat.
3. **No regression at mid/high bitrate:** 250k/500k/2000k KF Q and bits **byte-identical**
   to HEAD (the gate must not fire there — A1 must be a no-op when `target_bpm ≥
   near_lossless_bpm`). Guarded by the existing C1 unit tests
   (`budget_gate_keeps_kf_q_near_lossless_at_high_bitrate`,
   `budget_gate_does_not_bite_golden_fixture` in cbr.rs / `budget_gate_validation.rs`).
4. **2nd clip no-regression:** `foreman_320x256_60f.y4m` (real foreman variant, in
   corpus) shows the same direction (KF share down at low bitrate, no high-bitrate
   regression). **NOTE:** `testsrc_720p30` is NOT a usable 2nd clip — prior work
   (E6/E7) measured libvpx *saturates* on testsrc (PSNR not measurable, no overlap),
   so BD-rate is undefined there. The honest 2nd clip is the foreman variant.
5. **cross_decoder 4/4:** `cross_decoder_gate.rs` byte-exact vs vpxdec + ffmpeg,
   decode_failed = 0.
6. **No buffer underflow** beyond HEAD: `buffer_level` never more negative than
   `−maximum_buffer_size` (already clamped); A1 should make it *less* negative.

### 4.2 The NULL (pre-registered, and currently the LEADING hypothesis)
**NULL-A (front-loading):** *"Front-loading the KF further cannot improve BD-rate
without starving inter frames."* — Measured TRUE on HEAD (§1.2): rvp9 already
front-loads more than libvpx; more KF = worse overshoot = worse BD-rate (E7).
**The brief's lever is rejected by this NULL.**

**NULL-A1 (the redirect lever):** *"Shaving the KF at low bitrate does not improve
foreman BD-rate, because the freed bits land on inter frames whose prediction is so
weak (E9, ~26 dB, Q-independent) that the extra bits quantise toward zero and PSNR
doesn't move."* If NULL-A1 holds, **even the defensible KF-share reduction is inert**
and the whole KF-allocation lever is closed — all remaining BD-rate is in the inter
prediction path (Track B). This is a **plausible outcome** given E9 (inter PSNR is
Q-independent), and the design must report it honestly rather than ship an inert change.

### 4.3 Measurement (exact, reproducible)
- **Per-frame shape + KF byte-share + matched mean:** `diag_foreman_per_frame`
  (`crates\rvp9-encoder\tests\diag_foreman_per_frame.rs`), already prints per-frame
  q/bits/PSNR for rvp9 and libvpx and the TOTAL bits / actual-kbps / avg-PSNR.
  Run: `mdtimeout 560s cargo test --release -p rvp9-encoder --test diag_foreman_per_frame -- --ignored --nocapture`.
  Add `foreman_320x256_60f` as a 2nd clip and 500k to the existing `[25k,50k,100k,250k]`
  sweep (test-only edit, not production).
- **KF-only isolation:** `diag_foreman_kf` (KF q/bits/PSNR at 25/50/100/250/1000k).
- **BD-rate:** the existing quality/realtime gate (`quality_gate.rs` / `realtime_gate.rs`)
  + vpxenc cpu7 anchor at 100k/250k/500k.
- **Cross-decoder:** `cross_decoder_gate.rs`.
- **Unit (composition with C1):** the four `budget_gate_*` tests must still pass;
  add one A1 test asserting low-bitrate KF Q rises (e.g. 50k first-KF q ≥ 70) while
  250k stays q=4 (byte-identity proxy).

### 4.4 Metrics to report (per clip, per bitrate)
Per-frame PSNR curve (the f0…f31 shape, to see KF spike + GOP decay); matched-actual-
bitrate mean PSNR (rvp9 vs libvpx); KF byte-share %; achieved-vs-target kbps (overshoot
factor). Bitrates: 100k / 250k / 500k, foreman_cif **and** foreman_320x256_60f.

## 5. Risks + rollback + scope

### 5.1 Risks
- **R1 (highest): the lever is inert (NULL-A1).** Freed KF bits land on weak-prediction
  inters → no PSNR move. *Mitigation:* measure first (§4.3); if inert, **do not ship**
  — report and redirect to Track B. This is the most likely outcome.
- **R2: shaving the KF *hurts* a clip where the KF does propagate.** On low-motion
  content a sharp KF *does* carry the GOP; A1 would soften it. *Mitigation:* the 2nd
  clip + the bitrate-aware gate (only fires when `target_bpm ≪ near_lossless_bpm`,
  i.e. genuinely starved); keep A1 CBR-low-bitrate-local.
- **R3: byte-identity break at mid/high bitrate** if A1 leaks into the
  `target_bpm ≥ near_lossless_bpm` branch. *Mitigation:* A1 must be a strict subset
  of the existing CAP branch; the high-bitrate unit tests + golden fixtures guard this.
- **R4: the whole effort is mis-scoped.** The biggest risk is spending the BD-rate
  budget on the KF when the measurement says it's in inter. *Mitigation:* this HLD
  surfaces it for the lead before any code.

### 5.2 Rollback
A1 is a single gated branch in `cbr.rs::pick_q_and_bounds` (KF arm) + one constant.
Rollback = revert the basis-widening; C1 and f971c4e are untouched. Trivial.

### 5.3 Honest LOC / scope
- **Track A1 (if greenlit):** ~15–30 LOC in `cbr.rs` (bitrate-aware basis in the
  existing gate) + ~20 LOC unit test + test-harness 2nd-clip/500k additions (~30 LOC,
  test-only). No buffer-model change, no new struct fields, no VBR change. Small.
- **Track B:** out of scope here — separate HLD(s), much larger (E9 names 2–3
  picker-side fixes; GF/ARF boost is a multi-day port).

  is q=4 / 301 152 bits / **53.92 dB — sharper than libvpx (45.72 dB)** — and takes
  **~60% of the stream, not 20%.** rvp9 *over*-allocates the KF at mid bitrate; the
  "41.81 dB / 20%" figure matches the **pre-C1 (E7) code state**, not HEAD. C1 +
  f971c4e already fixed the under-allocation.
- **The dominant BD-rate loss is INTER, not KF:** rvp9 trails libvpx by 2.3 dB mean
  PSNR at 250k *despite* a sharper KF and *despite* spending 1.9× the rate budget.
  The sharp KF does not propagate — the GOP collapses to ~26–35 dB inter quality by
  frame 4 (E9: ~26 dB Q-independent prediction). This is the real lever.
- **"Front-load the KF more" is rejected (NULL-A, measured).** rvp9 already front-loads
  more than libvpx; pushing the KF higher worsens the rate overshoot and BD-rate (E7
  verdict). The only defensible KF-allocation change is the **inverse**: a
  bitrate-aware KF-share *reduction* at ≤100k (Track A1) to feed the starved inters.
- **Track A1 is small and composes with C1 by construction** (it widens C1's own gate
  basis at low bitrate; high-bitrate near-lossless and the byte-identity goldens are
  untouched). ~15–30 production LOC. But it may be **inert** (NULL-A1): the freed bits
  land on weak-prediction inters that quantise them away. **Measure before shipping.**
- **Pre-registered oracle:** `diag_foreman_per_frame` + `diag_foreman_kf` (real
  encode/decode, libvpx side-by-side) on foreman_cif **and** foreman_320x256_60f at
  100k/250k/500k; success = low-bitrate KF share → ~40%, BD-rate improves/flat,
  byte-identity at mid/high, cross_decoder 4/4, no underflow. **testsrc is unusable
  as a 2nd clip** (libvpx saturates → BD-rate undefined).

### Single biggest open question for the lead
**Do we still want a KF-allocation change at all?** The measurement says the KF is
already over-allocated and the BD-rate is in the inter path. If you want a KF change,
the *only* honest one is Track A1 (reduce low-bitrate KF share) — and it may prove
inert. **Should we instead close this HLD as "premise stale — KF allocation already
fixed by C1/f971c4e" and reopen the budget against the inter overshoot + prediction
path (Track B / E9)?** That is where the measured 2.3 dB lives.
