### An unresolved contradiction worth one cheap check

The 08-03 report implied the two Must/High bugs were gating the tree. I tried to confirm that and
could not — the evidence points both ways, so I am reporting the contradiction rather than
picking a side:

- `.deltic-integrate.toml` runs the **full** `cargo test --workspace --locked`; there is no
  narrowed affected-area gate that would structurally skip these tests.
- Yet **21 integrations** landed on 08-07/08-08, each of which had to pass that gate.
- `RVP9-BUG-KILN-00062` is only **conditionally** red: its test is `cfg`-gated on
  `rvp9_conformance_vectors`, and the vectors are gitignored and untracked, so a machine that has
  not run `scripts/fetch-vectors.sh` never executes it. The bug file concedes this.
- `RVP9-BUG-KILN-00020` has no such guard. `crates/rvp9-core/src/dsp/mod.rs:56` still rejects
  `bs=64`, and `crates/rvp9-encoder/src/lib.rs:7853` records that `max_intra_bsize` only caps to
  `Block32x32` at **speed 5+** — so production cpu-used 6–8 never reaches the `bs=64` path, but
  the `w_tpl` test at `speed: 4` should.

So `00020` is a genuine unfixed API-safety defect whose *live blast radius is smaller than the
record implies* (test-only at current production speeds), and `00062` reds the gate only on
vector-bearing hosts. **One `cargo test -p rvp9-encoder --test w_tpl` run settles it.** Until
someone runs it, "the gate is red" is unproven and should not be repeated.

### Where Stage 4a actually stands

**Speed — the first open exit, and closer than the tree admits.**

Freshest direct comparison against pinned vpxenc (Unit 722, 21 warmed pairs, CPU7 1080p):

| Clip | wall ratio | CPU ratio | median vpxenc/rvp9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `in_to_tree` | `1.141936x` | `1.193412x` | `5.510 / 6.292 ms` |
| `ducks_take_off` | `1.029866x` | `1.043827x` | `6.779 / 6.981 ms` |
| `old_town_cross` | `1.141975x` | `1.166442x` | `5.202 / 5.941 ms` |
| **Corpus geomean** | **`1.103299x`** | `1.132645x` | — |

Gate is `<=1.05x`; the journal states plainly "CPU7 still misses the final `<=1.05x` relative
speed gate." But the gap is **~10%, not ~30%** — several later journal summaries still cite Unit
680's `1.298683x` as "the last reliable direct ratio", which is stale by Unit 722. A skeptic
reading only those summary lines reproduces the pessimistic figure; I hit that exact trap and
caught it only by reading further. **Worth correcting in the journal**, because it makes the
remaining work look twice as large as it is.

Note the absolute times: 5.9–7.0 ms/frame at 1080p is far inside a 30 fps (33.3 ms) budget. The
open gate is **relative to libvpx**, not absolute real-time.

**The remaining deficit is almost entirely one identified cost.** Units 723–729 measured the
main-thread wait from the caller tile to the last worker at median `0.533 ms` / mean `0.566 ms`
per frame, and proved it is a content-stable final-picker imbalance (tile 1 finished last on
165/177 `in_to_tree` frames; tile 3 on 176/177 `ducks` frames). On `in_to_tree` the total deficit
is `6.292 - 5.510 = 0.78 ms/frame` — so that ~0.6 ms tail is roughly **three-quarters of the
whole remaining gap**. The campaign also ruled out the alternatives with counterfactuals:
removing every tile output copy would recover only `0.063 ms`.

That fully justifies Units 730–742. The compact-row restructuring is aimed precisely at the
dominant remaining cost, not at a vanity target.

**Quality — good, but not "solved", and the aggregate is fragile.**

confirmed against `crates/rvp9-bench/src/bin/bdrate.rs:145`):

| Clip | 720p | 1080p |
|---|---|---|

Three caveats that matter, and that I initially got wrong:

1. **The aggregate is outlier-dominated.** It is an arithmetic mean of three clips.
   Drop `ducks_take_off` and 720p becomes `-0.295%` and 1080p **flips to `+1.219%`** — rvp9
   losing. "rvp9 beats libvpx" is not supportable from this corpus.
2. **No per-clip figure meets the 0.5% release gate**, which is a closeness band in *both*
   directions, not a one-sided test. All six overshoot it. All six do clear the looser Stage 4a
   `<=2%` per-clip guard — `in_to_tree` 1080p at `+1.865%` only just.
3. **The numbers are VBR; Stage 4a is literally defined as 1-pass CBR** (`PLAN.md:530`). And the
   only checked-in BD-rate tool, `crates/rvp9-bench/src/bin/bdrate.rs:97`, hardcodes
   `--end-usage=cbr` with no VBR path anywhere in the repo. **The tool that produced the headline
   quality numbers is not in the tree**, so those numbers cannot be reproduced from a clean
   checkout. That is a real evidence-integrity gap, not a paperwork nit.

## Conclusion

The 08-03 report's picture is superseded. That pass observed a genuinely quiet moment — its own
report commit sits at the *oldest* end of the 636-commit block that landed the same day — and
concluded "stable means blocked". Both halves turned out wrong within hours.

What is actually true: this is a well-run, unusually well-instrumented performance campaign that
has closed the vpxenc speed gap from ~30% to ~10%, shipped the literal-720p/1080p capability that
makes the release gates measurable at all, and kept byte-exactness throughout — every unit records
release-binary and three canonical IVF SHA-256 hashes. The remaining deficit is diagnosed to a
single content-stable tile-completion imbalance worth ~0.6 ms/frame, which is most of the gap, and
Units 730–742 are aimed squarely at it.

The risks are not in the engineering, they are around it. Thirteen consecutive units have landed
as byte-exact ownership prerequisites behind `RVP9_COMPACT_RECON_ROWS=1`, all explicitly declining
a speed claim, on a path that is deliberately serial and therefore currently ~1–2% *slower*. That
is a defensible way to build a parallel refactor safely, but the payoff is entirely unbanked and
the runway is long. Meanwhile the campaign's evidence rests on three clips, one architecture, a
tool that is not in the repo, and no tracked ratchet — while `PLAN.md` tells any new agent none of
this happened.

The engineering is ahead of its bookkeeping. Today's cheapest wins are all in the bookkeeping.
