# Local release qualification

This runbook describes existing local gates. Running a command is not release approval. Keep a clean, recorded candidate and separate evidence for macOS Apple Silicon and native Windows x86-64. Use a task worktree and pass it as the tool's working directory; do not switch the trunk-holder. Build distributable artifacts only from the approved integrated source. Do not publish, tag, push or integrate as part of qualification.

## Acceptance boundary

Production-quality modes are single-stream CBR, denoiser CBR and spatial CBR, 8-bit profile 0, realtime CPU-used 6–8. VBR and temporal SVC keep the ceilings in README and PLAN and retain every correctness, speed, latency and safety gate. Frame dropping and EXT_RATECTRL remain unresolved roadmap requirements pending Arthur's explicit release-boundary decision.

The checked-in performance scripts enforce p50/p99 budgets of 33.333/50 ms at 720p30 and 1080p30, and 16.667/25 ms at 1080p60. PLAN §2 currently says 33/16 ms for the medians. Until that discrepancy is explicitly reconciled, report actual results against both and require the stricter 33/16 ms for the written §2 claim. Do not silently round a failing measurement into a pass.

## Check the oracles before real campaigns

Create a registered Deltic scratch for temporary files, logs and the Cargo target; export it as TMPDIR because the resume and timing self-tests require TMPDIR.

```sh
scratch="$(deltic scratch new --label rvp9-gate-tests)"
export TMPDIR="$scratch"
export CARGO_TARGET_DIR="$scratch/target"
export CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=1
run_check() {
  name="$1"; shift
  "$@" > "$scratch/$name.raw.txt" 2>&1
  status=$?
  printf '%s\n' "$status" > "$scratch/$name.exit.txt"
  return "$status"
}
failures=0
run_check production-self-test deltic timeout 180 bash scripts/qualify-production-encoder.sh --self-test </dev/null || failures=1
run_check production-resume-self-test deltic timeout 240 bash scripts/qualify-production-encoder.sh --self-test-resume </dev/null || failures=1
run_check production-timing-self-test deltic timeout 180 bash scripts/qualify-production-encoder.sh --self-test-timing </dev/null || failures=1
run_check spatial-self-test deltic timeout 60 bash scripts/qualify-spatial-svc.sh --self-test </dev/null || failures=1
run_check soak-unit-tests deltic timeout 900 cargo test -p rvp9-bench --bin soak --locked --offline -- --test-threads=1 </dev/null || failures=1
run_check bdrate-unit-tests deltic timeout 900 cargo test -p rvp9-bench --bin bdrate --locked --offline -- --test-threads=1 </dev/null || failures=1
exit "$failures"
```

The four qualification-script self-tests exercise stubbed observations and filesystem/error handling. The soak and BD-rate unit suites cover their focused regression paths; they do not prove production campaign results or finite BD-rate accuracy. Preserve every raw log and exit capture in the registered scratch, copy and hash them into durable evidence, then remove the scratch. RVP9-BUG-KIL-00107 (singular fits), RVP9-BUG-KIL-00109 (zero-work soak), RVP9-BUG-KIL-00112 (child deadlines) and RVP9-BUG-CRU-00001 (x86 scalar routing) are Fixed; run their focused regressions at the chosen candidate before relying on those paths. Record positive completed work for soak. Decoder forced-scalar conformance alone does not establish scalar encoder routing, and the x86 routing fix does not establish Arm scalar coverage.

`RVP9-BUG-FLU-00042` remains an open numeric blocker: the shared BD-rate solver accepts inaccurate finite results on narrow realistic PSNR curves. Repair and qualify the affected numerical tooling before expensive quality campaigns; inspect the separate standalone solver too. Singular-fit rejection does not settle numerical accuracy. `RVP9-BUG-FLU-00041` also leaves the spatial quality summary malformed: unquoted bitrate lists expand its CSV rows beyond the header. Preserve raw per-layer measurements and cross-decoder records, but do not accept that summary as a valid CSV or silently substitute a repaired result for the bug owner's fix.

The conformance script's forced-scalar labels are currently invalid on AArch64: native capability detection ignores `RVP9_FORCE_SCALAR` (`RVP9-BUG-FLU-00043`). Its macOS scalar-labelled runs do not provide scalar coverage. Keep native checks independent and leave the scalar gate incomplete until the owner repairs and verifies the mechanism.
