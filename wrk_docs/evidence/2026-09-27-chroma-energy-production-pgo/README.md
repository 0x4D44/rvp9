# Chroma-energy certificate: production-PGO measurements

The bounded early zero-chroma candidate is committed locally as 891ba61c and mirrored on Crucible as cf9282faf. Both copies of crates/rvp9-encoder/src/lib.rs have SHA-256 f26ab81338156b0710fc8db8d8f392617c029133417482e05e9504ff27ecc98a. The candidate uses the checked, 8-bit DctDct fast-quantizer energy bound in the [design note](../../2026.09.27%20-%20HLD%20-%20chroma%20zero-proof%20energy%20certificate%20hypothesis.md); every miss retains the existing transform proof. The new old-path differential test was deliberately made red and restored. Three focused encoder tests and the all-target encoder check passed on AArch64 and native Crucible x86.

Crucible built the candidate with the unchanged 28-row, 60-frame production-PGO recipe: [manifest](build-manifest.txt), [build wrapper](build-pgo-energy.sh), and [paired runner](screen-energy.ps1). Each row has its own identity, raw, and summary CSV here. The fresh candidate direct result remains above the ≤1.05× parity target. More importantly, the fresh control artifact measures 1.102646× libvpx while an [earlier artifact of identical shipped source](../2026-09-27-early-dual-tx-chroma-proof/README.md) measured 1.061826×. The shipped tree from f78e7d89 to c717a24a is byte-identical, but the earlier profile data and executable were not retained. The ~3.8% equal-clip difference is an artifact/campaign spread, not proven to be caused by PGO build variation: training-input hashes and host affinity, frequency, load, power, and thermal conditions were not captured in both campaigns. The fresh control was built and measured separately, not concurrently with the candidate/libvpx run, so subtracting their direct ratios would be unsound.

**Later decision:** the subsequent [shared-profile crossover](../2026-09-27-chroma-energy-shared-profile/README.md) found only 0.4% equal-clip gain under the control profile despite 6.5% under the candidate profile. A second [independent full-PGO build pair](../2026-09-27-chroma-energy-independent-pgo/README.md) then repeated the natural-build gain at 1.7% equal-clip CPU with exact output. That resolves the retention gate in favor of integration, while leaving the size of future PGO gains uncertain. The older integrated PGO executable was removed with its former worktree, preventing a direct binary-to-binary check against it. The conditional mathematical proof and source-level x86/AArch64 tests stand separately from this performance decision.
