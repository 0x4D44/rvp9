> **One-line thesis.** rvp9's ZEROMV over-use is **NOT** a cost-model problem and **NOT** a weak-NEAREST problem. The NEAREST candidate is derived byte-exactly with the decoder (verified). The bug is a **dedup divergence in the picker's candidate loop**: libvpx pre-populates `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]` *before* the mode loop and then **unconditionally skips any non-NEAREST mode whose MV equals NEAREST's** (`vp9_pickmode.c:2286-2289`), so when `NEAREST == (0,0)` the very first candidate `(LAST, ZEROMV)` is dropped and NEARESTMV wins. rvp9 guards that same skip on `mode_checked[NEARESTMV]` (`inter.rs:1643-1648`), which is **false at iter-0** because ZEROMV runs first — so ZEROMV is evaluated, becomes `best`, and an RD **tie** (identical MV ⇒ identical prediction, SSE, skip-decision) is resolved by the strict-`<` keep-incumbent rule (`inter.rs:1816-1819`) in ZEROMV's favour. This is a **fixable bug** of exactly the Phase-A kind, not the Lever-3b cost-model trap.

## 0. TL;DR for the lead (read this, then §2 and §6)

- **TRACTABLE — fixable bug, code-verified (not inference).** The mechanism is a 5-line correctness divergence in `pick_inter_mode`'s duplicate-MV skip, not a lambda/domain calibration. The in-code comment at `inter.rs:1638-1642` *claims* "the downstream picker cost-ranking still selects the libvpx-equivalent winner" — **that claim is false** for the ZEROMV-vs-NEARESTMV tie when `NEAREST == (0,0)`, which is the dominant case on foreman (E3: `nearest=(0,0)` on 2520/2520 sampled leaves).
- **NEAREST candidate quality is GOOD (not the Phase-A-style "weak candidate" failure).** The encoder builds `MvCandidates` via the **decoder-side** `find_mv_refs` (`lib.rs:3187-3212`) — the same `dec_find_mv_refs` the decoder runs, including `clamp_mv_ref`, `lower_mv_precision`, and the prev-frame-MV salvage (A18, gate-open by default). So NEAREST is byte-identical to what the decoder reconstructs. The candidate is not weak; it is correctly derived and then **discarded by the tie-break**, not by distortion.
- **Why NEAREST is so often (0,0): a self-reinforcing cascade.** Because the bug makes the picker emit ZEROMV on every (0,0)-NEAREST tie, the next frame's `find_mv_refs` reads neighbour MVs that are all (0,0) ⇒ NEAREST collapses to (0,0) ⇒ ZEROMV wins again. The bug poisons its own candidate source. libvpx breaks the cascade by always preferring NEARESTMV on the tie, so its neighbour grid stays populated with real tracked MVs (NEARESTMV 60-86%).
- **This is NOT Lever-3b.** Lever-3b is about the coef-rate-vs-pixel-dist domain mismatch on **residual-coded** blocks (the testsrc regression). The ZEROMV/NEARESTMV bug fires on **MV-tied** blocks where rate AND distortion are *identical* between the two modes — domain alignment cannot touch it, because there is nothing to weigh. The fix is upstream of the cost model.
- **Honest LOC + risk:** ~15-40 LOC in `pick_inter_mode` (pre-populate NEAREST's MV, make the dedup unconditional on the in-bounds NEAREST, keep the OOB-substitution guard). The risk is **not** the code; it is (a) byte-exact recon (the emitted mode changes ⇒ different bytes ⇒ cross_decoder is the hard gate), and (b) whether flipping ties to NEARESTMV actually moves BD-rate or merely relabels skip blocks. §6.4 pre-registers the NULL for (b).

## 1. Problem

### 1.1 The measured divergence (given, established-oracle)

From `notes/e1_block_stats.md` and `notes/_e1_postPhaseA.md` (foreman 256×256, 64 frames, cpu7 CBR; both rvp9 and libvpx bitstreams decoded through the **same** rvp9 decoder; mode % is area-weighted over inter frames):

| Operating point | metric | rvp9 | libvpx |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 25k | NEARESTMV % | **3.8** | **85.7** |
| 25k | ZEROMV % | **84.1** | **4.2** |
| 25k | skip=1 % | 79.0 | 97.0 |
| 100k | NEARESTMV % | 13.1 | 69.5 |
| 100k | ZEROMV % | 64.2 | 3.4 |
| 250k | NEARESTMV % | 23.1 | 59.9 |
| 250k | ZEROMV % | 38.7 | 3.0 |

rvp9 picks ZEROMV **~10-20× too often** and NEARESTMV **~3-20× too rarely**. The pattern is monotone: as q falls (higher bitrate), rvp9's ZEROMV share drops and NEARESTMV rises, but it never approaches libvpx's ratio. This is the trunk of the ~1.7-2.1 dB matched-Q inter gap: libvpx tracks motion with a cheap NEAREST predictor (near-zero residual, skip=1 97%), while rvp9 falls back to ZEROMV which (on actually-moving content) leaves a residual it must then code.

### 1.2 What is and isn't the cause (the measured-fact / inference split)

The brief poses three hypotheses. After reading the code, the verdict is:

- **NEAREST candidate quality — NOT the cause (code-verified).** The candidate is correctly derived. See §2.1.
- **Mode-RD ranking — THE CAUSE (code-verified).** A dedup divergence resolves MV-tied ZEROMV/NEAREST in favour of ZEROMV. See §2.2.
- **S2/domain mismatch — NOT the cause of *this* gap (measured + reasoned).** The `_e1_postPhaseA` numbers were taken with the frame-adaptive S2 gate ON (foreman classifies "natural", `frame_loop.rs:1836-1844`), and ZEROMV is *still* 38-84%. The domain mismatch (Lever-3b) acts on residual-coded blocks; the ZEROMV/NEAREST tie is rate- and distortion-identical, so S2/S3 cannot move it. See §2.4.

## 2. Diagnosis: fixable bug vs cost-model-blocked (the decision-critical section)

**Verdict: TRACTABLE. This is a fixable picker-dedup bug, structurally analogous to the Phase-A descent bug, NOT the Lever-3b cost-model trap.** The reasoning is below; the tractability verdict is the key deliverable.

### 2.1 NEAREST is correctly derived (FACT — code)

The encoder does **not** use a bespoke/weak NEAREST. At the per-leaf picker (`lib.rs:3187-3212`) it builds `MvCandidates` by calling `find_mv_refs(InterMode::NewMv, …)` for NEAREST and `find_mv_refs(InterMode::NearMv, …)` for NEAR, then `lower_mv_precision`. That `find_mv_refs` (`crates/rvp9-core/src/inter/mv_ref.rs:668`) is the **decoder variant** (`dec_find_mv_refs`, `block = -1`) — the *same function the rvp9 decoder runs* — including:
- the 8-neighbour spatial scan + sign-bias second walk (`mv_ref.rs:496-577`),
- the prev-frame-MV salvage (`mv_ref.rs:537-547, 580-596`), which **is** wired and gate-open by default (`frame_loop.rs:2599-2619`: gate collapses to "previous frame was a SHOW, non-KEY, same-size frame"),
- `clamp_mv_ref` finalisation (`mv_ref.rs:627-628`).

So rvp9's NEAREST is **byte-identical to the decoder's** ref-MV for the same leaf — this is the very property the drift-fix (`wrk_journals/2026.05.27 - JRN - inter-frame drift RESOLVED`) was built to guarantee. The candidate is not wrong or weak. **This rules out the Phase-A-style failure mode** (where the *derivation* was the bug). It is the opposite: a correct candidate discarded by a ranking bug.

### 2.2 The mode-RD ranking bug (FACT — code; this is the root cause)

ZEROMV and NEARESTMV both signal only a mode index (no MVD), so **MV-rate is not the differentiator** (the brief's framing is correct). On a leaf where the NEAREST ref-MV is `(0,0)`, the two modes have **identical MV ⇒ identical prediction ⇒ identical pixel SSE ⇒ identical `block_yrd` ⇒ identical skip decision**. The only difference is the mode-symbol cost `inter_mode_costs[ctx][idx]`. libvpx and rvp9 resolve this tie in **opposite** directions:

**libvpx (`vp9_pickmode.c`):**
1. `find_predictors` pre-populates `frame_mv[NEARESTMV][ref_frame]` *before* the candidate loop (`:1285-1287`).
2. The loop iterates `ref_mode_set` = `{(LAST,ZEROMV), (LAST,NEARESTMV), …}` (`:1239-1246`) — ZEROMV first.
3. At iter-0, the **unconditional** dedup fires (`:2286-2289`):
   ```c
   if (this_mode != NEARESTMV && !comp_pred &&
       frame_mv[this_mode][ref_frame].as_int == frame_mv[NEARESTMV][ref_frame].as_int)
     continue;
   ```
   With `NEAREST == (0,0)`, `ZEROMV.mv (0) == NEAREST.mv (0)` ⇒ **`(LAST, ZEROMV)` is skipped, never evaluated.** NEARESTMV at iter-1 becomes `best`. **Result: NEARESTMV is emitted.** ZEROMV is emitted only when NEAREST is genuinely non-zero (a real, different candidate).

**rvp9 (`inter.rs`):**
1. There is **no pre-population** of `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]`; the array is filled lazily as each candidate is evaluated (`:1585`).
2. The loop iterates the same `RT_INTER_MODES` order (`:1201-1210`) — ZEROMV first.
3. The dedup (`:1643-1648`) is **guarded on `mode_checked[NEARESTMV]`**:
   ```rust
   if mode != InterMode::NearestMv
       && mode_checked[ref_idx][InterMode::NearestMv.idx()]   // <-- false at iter-0
       && mv == frame_mv[ref_idx][InterMode::NearestMv.idx()]
   { skip_this = true; }
   ```
   At iter-0 (ZEROMV), `mode_checked[NEARESTMV] == false` ⇒ the skip does **not** fire ⇒ **ZEROMV is evaluated and becomes `best`.**
4. At iter-1, NEARESTMV runs with identical MV=(0,0). The new-best test is **strict** (`:1816-1819`): `biased_rd_cost < prev.best_rd_cost`. For the tie to flip to NEARESTMV, NEAREST's RD must be **strictly lower**.
   - **Skip-decision case (the bulk — skip=1 is 66-79%):** when `take_skip` fires, `final_rate = skip_true_cost` and `final_dist_rd = dist_skip`, both **mode-independent** (`:3001-3011`). So `this_rd` is *identical* for ZEROMV and NEARESTMV ⇒ strict `<` fails ⇒ **ZEROMV (incumbent) is kept.**
   - **Residual case (`!take_skip`):** `final_rate = rate + skip_false_cost`, where `rate` includes `mode_rate`. So ZEROMV wins iff `inter_mode_costs[ctx][ZEROMV] ≤ inter_mode_costs[ctx][NEARESTMV]`. The VP9 inter-mode tree (`adapt.rs:525-529`) places ZEROMV as the first-split leaf, so its cost is `cost(bit0)`; NEAREST is `cost(bit0) + cost(bit1)`. Whether ZEROMV is strictly cheaper depends on the adapted prob, but the tie still resolves *deterministically by mode-rate*, never toward NEAREST when costs are equal.

**The in-code comment at `inter.rs:1638-1642` explicitly asserts the ranking "still selects the libvpx-equivalent winner." It does not** — for the (0,0)-NEAREST tie it selects the *opposite* of libvpx. This is the bug. (Note: the comment is honest about *why* the guard exists — see R-OOB in §2.3 — but the conclusion that the outcome matches libvpx is the error.)

### 2.3 Why the guard exists, and why it's over-broad (the honest complication)

The guard was added for a **real** reason (`inter.rs:1624-1630`): rvp9's OOB-veto substitutes `Mv::ZERO` for `mvc_safe.nearest` when the real NEAREST is out of the picker's MV envelope (`:1236-1259`). Without the guard, that *substituted* zero would make `(LAST, ZEROMV)` look "equal to NEAREST" and get skipped — but in this case NEAREST is **not genuinely zero**, it was clamped, so skipping ZEROMV would be wrong and could empty the candidate set. The guard's intent — "don't skip ZEROMV against a *clamped* NEAREST" — is correct. Its **implementation** — "only ever skip after NEAREST has been evaluated" — is too broad: it also suppresses the skip in the *genuine* `NEAREST == (0,0) && in_bounds` case, which is exactly libvpx's skip case. The fix (§3) narrows the guard to the OOB case it was meant for.

### 2.4 Is S2 (skip reconciliation) interacting? (measured — no, not for this gap)

The `_e1_postPhaseA` measurement ran with the frame-adaptive S2 gate **ON** for foreman (foreman's HF energy ≥ threshold ⇒ "natural" ⇒ S2 active, `frame_loop.rs:1820-1844`). ZEROMV is *still* 38-84%. So honest-skip does not fix the ZEROMV over-use, because:
- The bug fires on MV-tied blocks where ZEROMV and NEARESTMV have **identical** rate and distortion in *every* domain (pixel or coef). S2 changes the skip *threshold*; it cannot break a tie between two modes that are bit-for-bit identical except their mode label.
- Lever-3b's testsrc mechanism (coef-rate over-stated ⇒ flee to ZEROMV) is about **residual-coded** blocks choosing ZEROMV over **NEWMV/motion**. That is a *different* ZEROMV inflation than the NEAREST tie this HLD targets. The two can coexist; this HLD fixes the tie, Lever-3b (if pursued) fixes the residual flight.

**Conclusion:** the binding constraint for the NEARESTMV/ZEROMV mode-mix is a correctness divergence in the dedup, sitting **upstream** of the cost model. Fixing it does not require touching lambda, `coef_rate`, or the dist domain. **TRACTABLE.**

### 2.5 The focused measurement that confirms (cheap, pre-code)

The diagnosis is from code; the brief asks for a focused measurement to confirm. The minimal one (does **not** require new production code — uses the existing throwaway-test pattern from E3 + the existing `pick_inter_mode_luma_all_costs` which returns all four candidates' costs, `inter.rs:635`):

> On foreman @100k/250k, for every leaf where rvp9's picker selects ZEROMV, dump `(nearest_mv, near_mv, in_bounds, zero_rd, nearest_rd, take_skip)`. **Prediction (to be confirmed):** the overwhelming majority have `nearest_mv == (0,0) && in_bounds && zero_rd == nearest_rd` (the tie), i.e. ZEROMV won purely as the iter-0 incumbent — **not** because ZERO had lower distortion than a non-zero NEAREST. A secondary bucket (non-zero NEAREST that genuinely loses on distortion) would indicate a *real* ZERO preference (the NULL, §6.4); the prediction is that bucket is small.

This is the disambiguator between "fixable bug" (tie incumbency) and "genuine ZERO preference" (cost model / content). It is ~20 LOC of diag in a `#[ignore]` test, run once.

## 3. Design — the fix

**Objective:** make rvp9's picker resolve the MV-tied ZEROMV/NEARESTMV case the way libvpx does — prefer NEARESTMV when the NEAREST ref-MV genuinely equals the candidate's MV — without re-introducing the OOB-substitution hazard the guard was protecting against, and without touching the cost model.

All changes are in `crates/rvp9-core/src/mode_decision/inter.rs`, inside `pick_inter_mode`, behind a config/env gate for landing safety (§3.4).

### 3.1 Pre-populate NEAREST's MV before the candidate loop (the libvpx structure)

Mirror `find_predictors`: before the `RT_INTER_MODES` loop, seed `frame_mv[ref_idx][NEARESTMV.idx()]` with the **in-bounds** NEAREST MV for each ref (LAST, GOLDEN). Use `last_mvc_safe.nearest` / `golden_mvc_safe.nearest` — but record a separate "NEAREST is genuinely zero (not OOB-substituted)" flag per ref, derived from `last_nearest_in_bounds` (`:1237`). This is the single fact the dedup needs.

### 3.2 Make the dedup unconditional on the *genuine* NEAREST (the fix)

Replace the `mode_checked[NEARESTMV]`-guarded skip (`:1643-1648`) with libvpx's unconditional form, narrowed to exclude the OOB-substitution case:

```text
skip iff  mode != NEARESTMV
          && mv == frame_mv[ref_idx][NEARESTMV]      // pre-populated in §3.1
          && nearest_in_bounds[ref_idx]              // genuine NEAREST, not a clamped-to-zero substitute
```

- When `NEAREST == (0,0) && in_bounds`: iter-0 `(LAST, ZEROMV)` is now skipped (libvpx behaviour) ⇒ NEARESTMV wins ⇒ **the cascade breaks.**
- When NEAREST is OOB (substituted to zero): `nearest_in_bounds == false` ⇒ the skip does **not** fire ⇒ ZEROMV stays evaluable (the guard's original, correct intent preserved). NEARESTMV is *also* OOB-vetoed in that case (`:1377-1380`), so ZEROMV is correctly the surviving zero-MV candidate.
- The existing zero-MV dedup loop (`:1599-1616`, libvpx `:2256-2268`) is left as-is — it is the complementary "duplicate zero-mv across already-checked modes" guard and is faithful.

This is the **whole behavioural change.** It is ~10-15 lines: a pre-loop seed + a 1-clause edit to the skip predicate.

### 3.3 Cost-model: explicitly UNCHANGED (the boundary with Lever-3b)

This HLD changes **only which of two MV-identical candidates wins the tie.** It does not touch `compute_rd_mult` (lambda), `coef_rate`, `coef_dist`, the S2/S3 gates, or the skip tie-break math. On the tied blocks, ZEROMV and NEARESTMV have identical rate/dist by construction, so the cost model has nothing to weigh — which is precisely why this is *not* Lever-3b and why it is safe to fix independently. (If the picker later needs the residual-flight fix, that is Lever-3b, separately gated.)

### 3.4 Gating + the stale comment

- Gate behind a new env (`RVP9_NEAREST_TIE_FIX`, default OFF for landing safety; flipped ON only after the oracle passes), so the default build stays byte-identical until the gate flips.
- Fix the misleading comment at `inter.rs:1638-1642` ("the downstream picker cost-ranking still selects the libvpx-equivalent winner") — it is the source of the misdiagnosis that this was cost-model-blocked. Replace with the corrected mechanism. Zero behaviour change from the comment edit.

### 3.5 Speed

Negligible. The change *removes* one `evaluate_candidate` call on tied blocks (ZEROMV is now skipped instead of evaluated), so it is net-neutral-to-faster. No new search, no new SIMD. Report `realtime_gate` ms before/after as a guard, but no budget risk is expected.

## 4. libvpx mechanism (faithful summary)

Sourced from the in-tree `third_party/libvpx/vp9/encoder/vp9_pickmode.c` (read directly, not via audit doc).

### 4.1 `find_predictors` (`vp9_pickmode.c:1256-1297`)
Per ref frame, before the mode loop: sets `frame_mv[ZEROMV] = 0`, `frame_mv[NEWMV] = INVALID`, then derives the candidate list. For the RT path it calls `mv_refs_rt` (`:60-153`) — the realtime ref-MV scan that also returns `const_motion[ref_frame]` — *unless* `use_prev_frame_mvs` (then `vp9_find_mv_refs`). It then calls `vp9_find_best_ref_mvs` (`:1285-1287`) which **populates `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]` and `frame_mv[NEARMV]`** (with `lower_mv_precision` + clamp). Finally `vp9_mv_pred` computes `pred_mv_sad` for reference masking. **Key fact: NEAREST's MV is known before the loop starts.** rvp9 builds the equivalent `MvCandidates` (via the decoder `find_mv_refs`) but never seeds `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]` ahead of the loop — the gap §3.1 closes.

### 4.2 `mv_refs_rt` NEAREST derivation (`:60-153`)
Scans the first 2 spatial neighbours (sub-block-MV aware), then the rest of `mv_ref_blocks[bsize]` until `refmv_count` is filled, then the sign-bias second walk for different-ref candidates, then the prev-frame base-MV (SVC). `const_motion = 1` unless 2 *distinct* same-ref MVs were found in the first 2 neighbours. rvp9 ports this faithfully: the candidate list via `dec_find_mv_refs`, and `const_motion` via `mv_refs_rt_const_motion` (`mv_ref.rs:714`). **NEAREST = `mv_ref_list[0]` after clamp/precision-lower** — exactly rvp9's `MvCandidates.nearest`.

### 4.3 The candidate loop + the dedup (`:2200-2410`)
Iterates `ref_mode_set` (ZEROMV-first per ref). The decisive lines for this HLD:
- **`:2259-2268`** — the "duplicate zero-mv" skip across already-`mode_checked` modes (rvp9 `:1599-1616`, faithful).
- **`:2286-2289`** — the **unconditional** "skip non-NEAREST mode whose MV == NEAREST's MV". This is the line rvp9 weakened with a `mode_checked` guard. **This single line is the NEARESTMV preference.** When NEAREST=(0,0), it deletes the ZEROMV candidate before it can win.
- `:2348-2364` — `block_yrd` + the skip tie-break (rvp9 `:2911-3011`, faithful; the domain question here is Lever-3b's, not this HLD's).
- The model RD (`model_rd_for_sb_y`), the filter search, `vp9_NEWMV_diff_bias`, the adaptive RD-thresh gate — all ported faithfully (rvp9 `:1653-1814`).

### 4.4 The one structural difference that matters
libvpx **prefers NEARESTMV over a tied ZEROMV by construction** (the unconditional `:2286` skip, fed by the pre-loop `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]`). rvp9's port preserved every *other* clause of the loop faithfully but altered this one for an OOB-safety reason, and in doing so inverted the tie. Restoring libvpx's behaviour (narrowed to the in-bounds case, §3.2) is the fix. The NEAREST+skip "fast preference" the brief asks about is exactly this: a tied NEAREST is the cheapest tracking predictor, and libvpx never lets ZEROMV steal it.

## 5. Risks + rollback

| # | Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Cross-decoder break.** The emitted mode changes (ZEROMV→NEARESTMV) on many blocks ⇒ different bytes ⇒ different bool-coder state ⇒ different `find_mv_refs` neighbours next frame. Recon must stay byte-exact through the decoder. | **HIGH** | cross_decoder 4/4 byte-exact + decode_failed 0 is the HARD gate. The picker and the writer both call the same `find_mv_refs`/`infer_inter_partition`, so the picked NEARESTMV is representable; the change is a *ranking* flip, not a new mode the emitter can't write. But this is a real test target — verify, don't assume. |
| R2 | **Cascade self-correction may take several frames** — once neighbour grids re-populate with real MVs, NEAREST stops being (0,0) and the mix shifts further. Could overshoot or destabilise rate control. | MEDIUM | Measure per-frame mode mix + bits (the `_e1_postPhaseA` per-frame table format). Watch for RC oscillation across the GOP. |
| R3 | **NULL: ties flip to NEARESTMV but BD-rate doesn't move** — if the tied blocks were genuinely going to skip with near-zero residual either way, relabeling ZEROMV→NEARESTMV changes the *histogram* (matches libvpx) but not the *bits/PSNR* (the mode-index cost delta is ~1 bit/block). | MEDIUM | §6.4 pre-registers this. The histogram matching is necessary-not-sufficient; the load-bearing oracle is matched-bitrate PSNR. If the histogram matches but PSNR is flat, the *real* lever is the residual-flight (Lever-3b) after all — escalate, do not claim victory on the histogram. |
| R4 | **OOB-substitution regression** — narrowing the guard wrong could empty the candidate set on edge blocks (the exact failure the guard prevented). | MEDIUM | The narrowed predicate keeps ZEROMV evaluable whenever `!nearest_in_bounds`; add a unit test (candidate set non-empty for an OOB-NEAREST edge leaf). Success requires **NEARESTMV area share UP and ZEROMV area share DOWN, toward libvpx**, at all four operating points. Report the full mode histogram (NEAREST/NEAR/ZERO/NEW/intra) + skip% rvp9 vs libvpx, before/after, per operating point and per-frame (to see the cascade settle).

### 6.3 Matched-bitrate PSNR (the BD-rate-facing metric — the real oracle)
At the stage-4a target bitrates, mean Y-PSNR rvp9 vs libvpx. **Success = mean-PSNR UP / BD-rate improved on foreman AND no regression on the 2nd natural clip AND no regression on testsrc.** Also report `e9_quality_gap` (matched-Q coef-bits / PSNR) per Q.

### 6.4 PRE-REGISTERED NULL (the honesty gate)
> **If the NEAREST tie fix moves the mode histogram toward libvpx (NEARESTMV up, ZEROMV down) BUT matched-bitrate mean-PSNR does NOT improve (within noise) — then the tied blocks were skip-equivalent either way, and relabeling them did not buy quality. The histogram matched cosmetically; the residual gap is elsewhere (the residual-flight / Lever-3b regime, or NEWMV/ME quality per E3). STOP. Do NOT claim a BD-rate win on the histogram alone. Report: "the ZEROMV/NEAREST tie is a real correctness bug and the fix makes the mix libvpx-faithful, but the matched-Q gain is < X dB because the tied blocks were already skip-coded — the remaining gap is [residual-flight / ME]." Escalate the sequencing question to Arthur.**

This guards against Goodhart-gaming the `e1` histogram. The histogram is the *symptom*; PSNR is the *disease*. We expect *some* PSNR gain (NEARESTMV keeps the neighbour grid populated ⇒ better NEAREST candidates next frame ⇒ less residual ⇒ the cascade compounds), but we pre-register the possibility that it's small.

### 6.5 Hard correctness gates (non-negotiable)
- **cross_decoder 4/4 byte-exact**, **decode_failed 0** across all gate streams (R1).
- Candidate-set-non-empty unit test for OOB-NEAREST edge leaves (R4).
- `cargo test -p rvp9-core --lib` (picker tests) + `cargo test -p rvp9-encoder --release` green; clippy clean on touched files.

## Exec summary (5 bullets)

1. **TRACTABLE — fixable bug, not cost-model-blocked (code-verified).** rvp9's picker weakened libvpx's *unconditional* "skip any non-NEAREST mode whose MV == NEAREST's MV" (`vp9_pickmode.c:2286-2289`) into a `mode_checked[NEAREST]`-guarded skip (`inter.rs:1643-1648`). Because ZEROMV is evaluated first, the guard never fires at iter-0, so on the dominant `NEAREST==(0,0)` tie ZEROMV becomes the incumbent and is kept by the strict-`<` new-best rule (`:1816-1819`) — the exact opposite of libvpx, which drops ZEROMV and emits NEARESTMV.
2. **NEAREST is correctly derived (rules out the Phase-A-style weak-candidate failure).** The encoder builds `MvCandidates` via the decoder-side `find_mv_refs` (`lib.rs:3187-3212`, incl. clamp, precision-lower, prev-frame salvage) — byte-identical to the decoder's ref-MV. The good candidate is discarded by the *ranking*, not by distortion. E3 already measured `nearest=(0,0)` on 2520/2520 leaves; this HLD explains *why* (a self-reinforcing cascade the bug creates).
3. **NOT Lever-3b / not the domain mismatch.** The bug fires on MV-tied blocks where ZEROMV and NEARESTMV are bit-identical except their mode label — rate and distortion are equal in *every* domain, so lambda/coef-rate calibration cannot touch it. `_e1_postPhaseA` confirms: ZEROMV stays 38-84% even with S2 frame-adaptive ON. The fix is upstream of the cost model.
4. **The fix is ~40 LOC, behind a gate:** pre-populate `frame_mv[NEARESTMV]` before the loop (mirroring `find_predictors`) and make the dedup unconditional, narrowed to the *in-bounds* NEAREST so the original OOB-substitution safety the guard provided is preserved. Net-neutral-to-faster (removes a candidate eval on ties).
5. **Pre-registered NULL is the honesty gate:** if the histogram matches libvpx but matched-bitrate PSNR stays flat, the tied blocks were skip-equivalent and relabeling them bought nothing — STOP, do not claim the BD-rate win on the histogram, and escalate to the residual-flight (Lever-3b) / ME (E3) levers. Hard gates: cross_decoder 4/4, decode_failed 0, no testsrc/2nd-clip regression, OOB-edge candidate-set-non-empty.

## Single biggest open question for the lead

**Do we expect this fix to *pay in BD-rate*, or merely to *correct the histogram*?** My honest read: this is a genuine, code-verified correctness bug (libvpx's NEARESTMV-tie preference, inverted), and fixing it WILL move the mode mix sharply toward libvpx — that part I am confident about. What I am **less** sure of is whether the matched-bitrate PSNR follows. The optimistic case (which I lean toward, ~60%): the fix breaks the (0,0) cascade ⇒ neighbour grids re-populate with real tracked MVs ⇒ NEAREST becomes a *good non-zero* predictor on subsequent frames ⇒ residual drops ⇒ the win compounds frame-over-frame, and libvpx's 60-86% NEARESTMV + 97% skip is *evidence* that this tracking is where its bit-efficiency comes from. The pessimistic case (the pre-registered NULL): the tied blocks were already skip-coded with near-zero residual, so the (0,0)-tie relabel is cosmetic and the real gap is the residual-flight (Lever-3b) or NEWMV/ME (E3). **The §6.1 confirmatory dump + the §6.4 PSNR gate disambiguate cheaply** — which is why I recommend running the dump *before* writing any fix code, and want your agreement that "histogram-matches-but-PSNR-flat" is an acceptable (and informative) outcome, not a failure to be tuned around.
